# Architecture

Cape Sorrow is a game on [`@csarkosh/engine`](https://github.com/csarkosh/engine), which draws the world, runs the
netcode and the deterministic simulation, and owns the page. The game gives the engine's shell one page definition and
one identity, and lays three things of its own over the engine's world: a generated port town, one car to drive in it,
and the title page. The game's rules are the engine's walking and the car's. This file says what is true of the
repository today, in the order the pieces depend on each other: the engine and what the game hands it, the town, the car,
the title page, hosting, and what is still scaffold and unfinished.

## The engine and what the game hands it

The game installs the engine as a release's tarball (the address in `client/package.json`; it is on v0.2.38). The engine
makes the world from a seed: the coast (the engine's `olympic` terrain, with its cove, its highway and its massif), the
forest and the clutter on it, the weather and the sky. A game registers the coast with a place laid over it, and the
world is then the engine's coast with that place's ground.

`client/src/main.ts` registers the shipped files and the terrain (`terrain.ts`, which imports `coast.ts`, which registers
the coast with `CAPE_PLACE`: see "The ground the game lays over the coast"), then calls the engine's `startShell` with
`CAPE_PAGE` (`client/src/page.ts`), a `PageGame`:

- `game`: the game a session is of (`EMPTY_GAME`, `client/src/game.ts`). Its renderer is the engine's, made with the town's renderer part (`townParts`), its start is the town's (`townStart`), and its rules (`WALK_RULES`, `client/src/sim.ts`) are the engine's walking and the car's: `spawn` searches out from the town's start for the first place a hull stands free, `newPlayer` gives each player a ride, `held` and `move` are the car's (a player getting in or out is held, a seated player is moved by the car), `constrain` is the town's buildings and the cars as walls, and `hostStep` is the car's step on the host (see "The car"). The game's `actions` name the two buttons the car uses (interact and the lamp), its audio is the engine's with the car's voice added, and its half of a session (`createCapeSide`) adds the line at a car's door and the `carhome` command to the day's clock.
- The game's `look` (`CAPE_LOOK`, `client/src/look.ts`), carried by `game`: the part of the engine's look the game changes, named Harbour, a cold, clear, wet working port at midday under high cloud. The engine's own look is Day Hike's, a landscape in oils; a town is read by its edges, its lettering and its paint, so the paint filter and the softening of what is far are off, the frame is half again as bright, the grade's palette is the game's own (blue-black shadows, a middle left almost alone so that brick and paint keep their colour, a slightly warm white), and a session starts at midday under damp weather with more cloud than sun. The grade follows the day: a palette and an exposure at four heights of the sun (night, dusk, late afternoon, midday), each for a clear sky and for full cloud, which the engine mixes between at every hour. Over the picture lies a finish that is the same at every hour: fine film grain, a faintly darker edge to the frame, the light barely gathered into bands, and a thin grey line where one thing ends against another, found by the engine from the scene's depth and not from the picture, so a brick wall's mortar draws none. The line begins at a step of half a part in a hundred of the distance behind it and is full at two and a half, half the engine's own, so that a sill or a casing is drawn from across a street; paint on a wall lies flush under its depth rank and draws none. Far off the line thins (`lineNear`, `lineFar`, `lineFarBand`, `lineFarWeight`): within 40 m it is as said, and from there to 600 m, eased along the logarithm of the distance, a step must be up to twenty times deeper to draw and the line fades to half its strength, so that a far forest is tone with a quiet edge and a mountain keeps one light contour, where each crown and fold used to be outlined. The look also sets the ground under the grass (`sward`): the line darkens every blade, so the terrain inside the blades' reach is pulled all the way to a floor as dark as they are, and ground the blades have not reached yet does not show pale. The look also asks for the engine's deeper sand (`sand`, engine v0.2.25: the beach's sand and pebbles read without visible repetition, varied in colour at three scales, with tide lines by height, darker hollows, ripples near the water and a ragged, graded wet edge), and the place asks for what lies on the beach and for its shape (`CAPE_PLACE.beach`: a wrack line of kelp, cobbles, small driftwood and dune grass; a storm ridge, cusps, bars and channels that hold pools, hummocks), and says which ground it paves (`pavedGround`, the highway's bed: the engine's patches of finer ground beside steep banks leave it out, or they drew the engine's painted road over the game's). The low tier has no grade and none of the chain's screen passes, so it takes the weather, the hour and the exposure and neither the palette nor the finish's grain, edge and bands; the line it does draw (engine v0.2.23: one pass after the scene, from the scene's depth, by the same measure and thresholds), coarser there because that tier draws fewer pixels and has no anti-aliasing over time to steady it.
- `side`: the game's own of the page (`createCapePage`), which the shell calls at the page's own points: it shows the title page, repaints it, shows a panel by its address, and keeps the mode. Nothing plays before a session starts.
- `identity` (`client/src/identity.ts`): the game's name, its word for a session (`case`), what a nameless player is called (`Detective`), and the prefix of everything it keeps in the browser (`capesorrow`).
- `away`: what the page for a visitor whose browser cannot play shows of the game: no still, and the same credits the Credits screen shows.
- `protocol`, `worldWorker`, `statusLine`, `scene`, `probeState`: the session's wire version, the worker that makes its world (`world.worker.ts`, handed the town's start and the chunk cells its first spawn reads, `townSpawnCells`), a line of status, the staged scenes (none), and the state the tier check measures the renderer on.

## The town

The town is simulation code in `client/src/sim/town/`: every machine computes the same town from the world's seed, with
nothing sent over the wire. The folder's rules (the arithmetic allowed, no trigonometry, bearings as triples, one random
stream per stage, `TOWN_GEN`) are in `AGENTS.md`, and `client/test/architecture.test.ts` and `client/test/town/banned.test.ts`
enforce them.

### The site

A plan is made from a `TownSite` (`site.ts`) and nothing else of the ground: the town's own seed (the world's, mixed with
which town this is), an anchor on the highway's centre line, and, in the town's own frame (origin at the anchor, +x
inland, +z north, metres), the shoreline's x and the highway's x sampled every 16 m along z, 1008 m either way of the
anchor, how far inland of the highway the town may reach (480 m), and a grid of the coast's own heights (8 m apart, out to 1.1 km inland and along the shore) that roads out of town are routed over and the land is judged on. The samples reach 1008 m either way of the anchor (the town's blocks reach 600 m, its outlying buildings further). `siteFor(worldSeed)` (`siteFor.ts`) reads it off
the engine's coast at the cove: the highway's line, and for each sample the shore found by a seaward scan from the highway
and then a fixed number of bisection steps, so every machine finds the same bits. It reads the coast's own ground, as it is
without a town laid over it (`coastGround`), so the site does not move when the town's ground is laid. Where a headland stands
at a sample the shore is the headland's tip; where the cove opens it is the cove's. There is one site, at the engine's cove. While the site is read the cliff bands are kept off a generous rectangle, so that the grid is the ground without them, as the roads out and the made ground lay it.

**The land** (`land.ts`) is derived once per site from the grid: for each cell its height and slope and whether it is buildable (no steeper than 14 percent, above 0.5 m, off the highway's bed, off the railroad's corridor, not on a headland's tongue), and the buildable hectares within the town's reach. The coast's own flats between the water and the highway stand 0 to 2 m, and the made ground lifts them to 2.5 m at the water's edge, so the height rule tests only that a cell is land and not tideflat awash. A site with no grid (the synthetic coast) has a flat land. Reading the site takes about 45 ms on a developer's machine and the land about 1 ms.

### The plan

`planTown(site, kind)` (`plan.ts`) returns a plain-data `TownPlan`: the history, the plats and the seam between them, the
streets and their ends, the roads out of town, blocks, lots, buildings, the landmarks, the made ground (`fill`), the `quay` and the `spawn`. The stages run in a
fixed order, each on a stream of its own: names, history, plats and streets, lots, buildings, landmarks. The history is drawn
first (`port/history.ts`) and every part of the form is derived from it, so that a difference between two towns has a cause a
player can name. `planFor(worldSeed)` (`planFor.ts`) is the plan on the world's own site, cached. A kind of town is a recipe
(`recipes.ts`) and a folder beside `port/`; the shared code never asks the kind, and only the large port exists. `TOWN_GEN` is
the generator's version (11) and is part of every plan.

On the real coast the shore runs near north to south, the highway stands 70 to 270 m inland of it, and at the cove two narrow
headlands run out to sea, so the cove is a natural harbour. The old plat stands at the cove behind a straight quay, partly on
made ground; the land company's compass plat wraps around it; the mill and the public port's terminal take the flats' shore;
the highway, which the engine paints and the town paves where it runs through (see "How the town is drawn"), runs through, and blocks keep clear of it. A plan has 20 to
55 blocks and 150 to 420 buildings (`client/test/town/size.test.ts`): the land and the history's budget set it, as the next section says.

**The town is laid where the land allows, outward from where it began** (`port/layout.ts`, `port/growth.ts`). The old plat stands at the best landing on the cove (of the places along it a window of columns can stand with water for a quay, the one with most buildable land behind it), to the bearing the history gives it, but only as many of its blocks as the land takes (a block needs 60 percent of its ground buildable, a mean slope of 14 percent or less, and its mean height within 4.5 m of the graded plane the made ground will be: where the land takes fewer than four, the four best stand). The history gives the town a SIZE, a budget of blocks: 28, and up to 14 more for how long the town had to grow (a founding in 1889 adds none, one in 1851 all), 6 and up to 4 more where the boom was not cut short by the panic of 1893 (a peak in 1900 adds none, 1912 all), and 3 for a county seat, clamped to 26 to 55. The company's grid is laid over the whole of the land the town could reach (600 m either way along the shore, 480 m inland of the highway), and the budget is spent on its blocks one at a time, always on the cheapest next to the town: a cell's price is its distance on foot from the core (along the blocks taken, in metres), 8 m for each percent of its mean slope, 120 m for the whole of it being unbuildable, less up to 45 m for standing on the highway (falling to nothing 70 m off it) and up to 30 m for standing on the water (60 m). A cell less than 60 percent buildable, steeper than 14 percent, or off the graded plane by more than 4.5 m is never taken; where a hump or a gully stands in a block's place the block is left as woods (`plan.woods`, with its reason, the land's or the budget's) and the grid goes round it, and two parts either side of the cove are possible where that is where the land is. The budget of blocks is held as well to the town's houses (`growth.ts`, `lotBudget`; every number of the change is in `port/sizing.ts`, authored): growth stops when the lots the blocks taken would hold (their area over 520 m2 a lot) reach 330 and, for each block the history's budget stands over 26, 3 more, so that a town's lots come to about the buildings it holds (the game's town: 28 blocks, 317 lots, 291 buildings) and the grid does not run on over land the houses do not reach. The business street is short: a town the panic of 1893 stopped has one block of shops either side of the main street (the block on one side of the wharf street, its dock side when the founding year is even), a town that kept its boom one more in the row nearer the main street, where the land lets them; the dock district is the one block of the quay's row a block or two past the wharf street (it was every such block); the highway strip takes 30 percent of the blocks beside the highway (it was 65); a tavern is a few in a hundred shop units (the counted towns' 3.7 percent). The blocks the business street no longer takes are houses, so the game's town holds about 210 houses to 42 shop units; the town's fill raises every district's chance of a building until 280 stand or 88 percent of the lots do, and the cap past which houses thin out is 340 to 420. Streets are the grid's lines pruned to the blocks that exist (`port/ends.ts`) and then, once the buildings stand, cut back to what the buildings serve (`port/trim.ts`: from each free end inward to the first stretch with a building within 20 m of it or the first junction, a street that serves nothing dropped where the network stays whole, an alley dropped where under 55 percent of it has a building near; the stretches cut away are the unbuilt plat, whose lots stay and whose streets are gone), so that about 10 percent of the town's streets have no building near, and where a street would climb more than 15 percent over 16 m on the coast's own ground it is cut there and the stretch is a public STAIR (`port/stairs.ts`, a `stair` street of 3.5 m of steps in the street's right of way; 0 to 9 a town over forty worlds). Where the town's blocks end the town thins out (`port/outskirts.ts`): single buildings on lots of their own, each a block of one lot (`outskirts` district) and each with a reason (`plan.outskirts`): a gas station and a motel on the highway where the last blocks end, a tavern, a church, houses on large lots, a junkyard, a shake mill, a volunteer fire hall, fewer the further out (0.8 chance of one at the town's edge falling to none 560 m out), and a few along the first 240 m of a road out; each on land the plan can build on, off the railroad's corridor. The railroad's main line (`rail.ts`, 16 m seaward of the highway) is kept clear (no block, lot or building within 5 m of it); streets cross it at grade (`plan.rail.crossings`), and the town's own track is a SPUR (`plan.rail.spurs`, `port/spur.ts`): where the history has a railroad it leaves the main line beyond the end of the town on the mill's side, curves to the strip between the water and the blocks, runs past the mill and along the quay and on to the terminal, every bend an arc of 70 m radius or more. The depot stands in the block beside the main line nearest the core. The blocks keep clear of the main line's bank as well as its corridor (`railBank.ts`: the bank's width grows with how far the highway's grade line stands over the made ground at the line, so blocks stand 24 to 50 m from the highway's centre), a street that would end in the corridor is run on across it to the highway (a recorded crossing) or cut back to its edge, and no outlying lot stands on the sea's side of the highway, where it would have to cross the line to be reached. Where the main line stands higher than 10 m at both ends of the town (the highway climbs a headland there) the town has no spur. The spur's corners are rounded with arcs of 85 m and, where the legs are too short, relaxed until none bends tighter than 65 m; its points are 8 m apart.

**The downtown** (`port/downtown.ts`; `plan.downtown`, the ids of its blocks; `plan.places`, `port/places.ts`). Every business of the town stands in one connected set of blocks, and nowhere else: the shops, offices, taverns, restaurants, banks, hotels, motels, boarding houses and apartments, the lodge hall, the school, the hospital, the gas stations and garages at its edge on the highway, the big-box store, the post office and the municipal building. Houses and mill housing, the mill and its yard, the industrial warehouses, the silos, the junkyard, the shake mill, the churches, the fire hall, the harbour's own (the quay, terminal, piers, cannery, dock warehouses) and the customs house and courthouse stay where the old plan put them (the courthouse is a lot of the downtown's outer blocks where the town is the county seat). THE RULE (authored, not researched): two blocks are neighbours when both stand beside one street (the highway counts, for the old plat stands either side of it) and are no more than 30 m apart (60 m across the highway); the downtown starts from the old core (the business street's blocks) and the dock block by the quay, takes the blocks between them by the shortest way along the streets, and grows outward one neighbouring block at a time, nearest the core and the dock first, until its blocks hold the lots the old plan gave to shops, the dock and the highway strip, and the rows the landmarks need (each block has two rows, one landmark in each). Up to two added blocks beside the highway are the "highway strip" district (the gas stations, motels, garages, the big-box store); every other block of it is "core" (the dock block stays "dock"), so everything that reads the district (the walkers, the lamps' and sidewalks' notion of the core, the shop signs, the paving) follows it. The blocks between the old core and the dock, and every block added, are the downtown's outer blocks (`BlockMeta.edge`, where one has a street to a block outside it): built thinner, with a few houses, apartments and vacant lots, so its edge is ragged, and densest at the core, the dock and the highway. A town with no old plat has no old core: its downtown begins at the block nearest where the core would be. The streets and the blocks are the old layout's (nothing of `layout.ts` moved but each block's district, and a street beside shops takes the shops' sidewalk and curb); the outskirts (`outskirts.ts`) hold no gas station, motel, tavern or garage any more. EVERY TOWN HAS, OPEN FOR BUSINESS, IN THE DOWNTOWN: the municipal building (a landmark, reserved with the others, with `ensureMunicipal` as the fallback that makes a building of the downtown it where its lot gave none), a lodging (`buildings.ts` opens a hotel, motel or boarding house of the downtown, or makes a shop of it one; `business/index.ts` opens its bay, and where no hotel's lobby and no motel is open there, the broadest open rooming house of the downtown takes the trade "inn", so the place is always a hotel, a motel or an inn, where a room is taken by the night), a bar (a tavern building, the same way) and a cafe (a bay of the downtown that holds the trade, opened, or a shop's bay that takes it); `plan.places` names each, `{ building, unit }` (the office has no unit: the whole municipal building), and `client/test/town/downtown.test.ts` holds all four, open, over sixty-four seeds. THE DOWNTOWN IS BUILT UP: no block of it has under 65 percent of its lots built (a vacant or boarded building counts; a lot that will not fit its building is given a smaller one, and the draws' empty lots of a thin block are built in the order of their draws until the share is met), the strip's highway side (a lot within 50 m of the highway) holds the motel, gas station, garage, big-box store and restaurant and the lots behind it shops, apartments and a boarding house (its lots are 18 to 40 m wide), an outer block holds shops, apartments and boarding houses ahead of houses (a house is at most a third of its buildings), and the downtown holds at least eight lodging and eight apartment buildings (shops of its outer blocks are made them where the draws gave fewer). The hotel, the bank, the lodge hall and the municipal building stand on core blocks, never on the strip. The municipal building's front is a pair of doors 2.3 m wide centred under its portico with two steps, windows kept 4.4 m clear of them, a name board of 0.4 m letters over the portico and boards of 0.26 m letters for "CITY HALL" and "SHERIFF" beside the door, in cream on dark or dark on cream; `plan.places.office.door` is `{ s, width }` along its front frame, the door the player will enter by. To keep a world under the game's memory limit (the played world's town stays under 1,605,000 vertices), a town past `TOWN_CAP` (296) buildings loses up to 8 houses of its big hill blocks (the company's plat inland of the highway, 6500 m2 and more, near the town, where the school and the hospital stood).

**What stands beside each street, and how each lot is kept** (`port/edges.ts`, `port/yards.ts`; `plan.edges` by street id, `plan.yards` by lot id; each on a stream of its own). The history decides, as it did in the towns of this coast, and every number is authored (the research found none). A street's ERA is `core` (the main street, the avenue, streets between shops and the civic blocks: a sidewalk on both sides all the way, 3.5 to 4 m wide, kept up), `old` (the old plat's house streets: 88 percent of sides have any, a gap of 5 to 25 m about every 100 m, worn), `later` (the company's plat: none 35 percent, one side 45, both 20; a sidewalk may stop mid-block where the money ran out) or `edge` (the later plat's last streets, over 400 m from the core: none 60 percent, one side 30, both 10). A sidewalk is poured in RUNS of 18 to 60 m, each its own decade (1900 to 2020, weighted to the booms and the 1950s) and wear (its age, the era and a vacant lot beside it), each with a joint spacing of 1.35 to 1.8 m. A side has a CURB with its sidewalk in the core and the old plat, mostly in the later plat and rarely on the edges; the curb stands 15 cm over the gutter where the street was rebuilt and each overlay of asphalt buried 3.5 cm of it, to 5 cm: the roadway has one to four stretches of PAVING, each with a decade, a curb height and a wear, so the curb steps down where the street was overlaid. A side with no curb has a gravel shoulder 0.9 to 1.6 m wide and, on more than half of the later and edge streets, a shallow ditch. A SHOP'S DOOR OPENS ONTO A SIDEWALK whatever the era: after every street's own draws, every stretch of a grid street's side that a business or civic front stands on (storefront, tavern, restaurant, office, bank, hotel, lodge, depot, church, school, hospital, motel, garage and the rest with a door on the street) is given a sidewalk and a curb for its frontage and on to the nearest junction or neighbouring run (60 m at most), poured in its own decade (`addShopWalks`; a test over 40 seeds asks every unit door of `plan.uses` for a sidewalk run at its station). Over the 12 worlds of the pass (and 40 seeds in `test/town/edges.test.ts`): sidewalk on 100 percent of core street sides, 92 percent of the old plat's, 48 percent of the later plat's and 24 percent of the edges'. A lot's YARD is `mown`, `shaggy`, `tall`, `bare`, `mossy`, `brush` (or `paved`, in a paved district) from its building's state and era, the district and a roll of its own: a vacant lot is tall grass, brush or shaggy, a ruin brush, a vacant building shaggy or tall or mossy, the lots of the outskirts barer; a house lot has a drive about half the time (gravel, dirt two-track, or a concrete ribbon) and a front walk six times in ten as broken as the yard is neglected.

How the history decides the form:

| In the history | In the plan |
|---|---|
| What the town was founded for (sawmill, cannery, customs act, railroad bet) and when | The town's name, the founder's, and with a customs act a customs house among the landmarks |
| What the first plat was laid to: the wagon road that became the highway, or (where the highway runs near north) the line of the first wharf or a railroad's survey | The old plat's bearing: the highway's own line, or one 22 to 37 degrees off the compass; so the highway is the old main street, or cuts across the old blocks |
| The second plat, a land company's or a railroad's, laid to the compass | The compass plat wrapped around the old one; where the two bearings differ by 25 degrees or more, a seam where the grids do not line up |
| A railroad, and when | A spur from the main line along the flats' shore and the quay, between the water and the town, and a depot beside the main line; none without a railroad |
| A fire, and whether it took the waterfront too | A core of the later era and material (brick, or concrete after 1921), the main street widened; without a fire an older core, some of it in wood |
| The 1893 bust | More vacant buildings, and a boom-era hotel never filled |
| The county seat | A courthouse on the hill if the town is the seat, and none otherwise |
| The public port after 1911, what it handles | A terminal on the flats |
| The mill: which side, when it opened, closed or working, cleaned up or not | The mill's site on the north or south flats; a closed mill's site has no open mill building, a cleared one fewer buildings than a fenced one |
| The present-day layer | The core's vacant share, a tsunami refuge on the hill or not, a strip of motels and big stores along the highway or not |

The streets are the two plats' grids, the wharf street, the rail and alleys where a block has them. Rights of way are those of a small
Northwest town (`ways.ts`): an ordinary street 14 to 16 m with a roadway of 7.5 to 9 m, sidewalks of 1.7 m and a grass verge between
sidewalk and curb (a sidewalk in its place between shops and on the working waterfront); the main street 18 m with 11 m of roadway and
sidewalks of 3.5 m (20, 12 and 4 after a fire); the one avenue 20 m with 12 m of roadway and a painted median; alleys 4 m of gravel with no
sidewalk; nothing is wider than 12 m of roadway beside the highway's 11 m bed. The blocks keep their centre-line sizes, so lots gain the
difference. A grid street runs only as far as the last block it serves (`port/ends.ts` cuts every stub past its last intersection or fronting
lot, with 4 m past the last lot); each end that is no junction is named in `plan.ends` as a landing at the water or a made end, a turnaround or a
barricade. A few roads leave town (`plan.roads`, `port/roads.ts`): from the grid's inland ends a county road to the cemetery on the hill (every
town) and on most an old logging road to a landing, a gravel pit or a water tank, and, where a street on the flats side reaches the grid's end and
there is room, a road along the shore's flats to a landing, a gate or a pit (none of the forty tested seeds has one). Each road is stepped across the
site's ground grid in 12 m steps, the heading turning by exact Pythagorean rotations, costed by the grade along and across it, held to 9 percent a step
and 7 percent over the last 24 m (loosened only where no road is found), and narrows from paved to gravel to two wheel tracks; its destination is a
landmark of no building (`Landmark.at`) and a sentence of the history. Blocks are cut by the shore and the highway; a district's lot rule
(`port/rules.ts`) cuts its lots, with real widths (commercial lots 7.5 to 15 m, house lots 15 m); each lot is built on or left empty, with a kind, an era, a material, a roof and a state (open, vacant or
ruin) that follow from its district and the history. Landmarks reserve their blocks before the lots are cut and are bound to
the buildings that take them afterwards: the municipal building (shared by the sheriff and the town's government: a civic building of the 1930s with a cornice, a portico and a flagpole, on the main street's side of a block of the downtown, with the town's name on a board over its door and a smaller board each for "CITY HALL" and "SHERIFF"; always landmark 0, and the player's office), the hotel, the bank, a lodge hall, a depot, the port's terminal, two churches, the high school, the
hospital, and as the history says a customs house, a courthouse and a tsunami refuge. The hotel, bank, lodge hall, municipal building, school, hospital and courthouse each take one of a downtown block's two rows (a block carries two reservations, one a row); the churches, the depot, the terminal and the refuge stand outside it. Grids are turned to bearings that are
Pythagorean triples (`bearings.ts`), so a plat's rotation is exact arithmetic.

**Junctions** (`junctions.ts`, `junctionShape.ts`; `plan.junctions`; its figures are the published research "How a small-town street intersection is built", the section named beside each in the code's comments). A junction is found where two streets of the grid meet (their centre lines cross, or one ends within the other's right of way) or a street ends within the highway's bed (the bed's edge, `ROAD_BED_HALF`, is a leg of its own with no pavement of ours); meetings within 3.5 m are one junction. Each has its LEGS, counter-clockwise, each a ray with the street's roadway, walk and width (`mouth`: where the leg's roadway begins; `bandLeft`, `bandRight`: where its curb, verge and sidewalk begin on each side), and its CORNERS, each a curb return (`radius`, tangent points `ta` and `tb`, centre `c`, the point `v` where the two curbs meet, and `lot` where the two lot lines meet) or a straight wedge (a tee's through side) or a sharp corner. Its KIND is a four-way (or a compound of more legs), a bend (two streets that end on each other's ends), a tee (with its stem), a skew (a four-way crossing more than 15 degrees off square: `skew` is the sine of that angle), a highway junction, or one of four that share no pavement: an alley's mouth (a driveway apron), a road end (a street's end where a road out goes on, with where the pavement ends), a stair, a rail crossing (crossbucks and stop line from the line's centre). Radii are 10 ft (3.0 m) between neighbourhood streets, 20 ft (6.1 m) with the main street or the avenue, 30 ft (9.1 m) to the highway's edge; authored: an obtuse corner keeps a square one's tangent and takes the radius that gives it, an acute one's tangent stops at 10 m, and two junctions so close that their returns would meet are `tight` (every corner 3.0 m), and where that is not enough they are one compound junction. CONTROL: none between quiet residential streets crossing within 15 degrees of square, STOP on a tee's stem, on the lesser street of a skewed crossing or of a marked one, and on every street meeting the avenue, the main street or the highway, and all-way stops at the one or two busiest corners of the core; there are no signals. Marked crosswalks are on the main street's and the avenue's legs, at the school's and the hospital's corners, and across the highway at the junctions of the core; every other crossing is unmarked. ### The harbour

The made ground along the quay is one convex polygon for each stretch of the quay with water seaward (`plan.quay.wet`), with collinear quay-side edges and no overlap, so the bank along the quay is one smooth slope. `plan.decks` lists every deck over the water (the quay's apron along each wet stretch, piers, the platforms of sheds on piles, the wharf street, and the boat basin's floats and gangways) as an oriented rectangle with the height of its top (`DECK_TOP` 2.6 m, floats `FLOAT_TOP` 0.4 m, a gangway's from one to the other): the one list the walking and the drawing both read. `plan.harbour` (`port/harbour.ts`, its own random stream) holds the rest, decided by the history:

- **The basin and its boats.** One or two main floats parallel to the quay, 22 m out, each reached by a gangway from the apron, with finger floats and slips of three classes. A cannery town or a seafood port keeps a full basin of working boats; a mill, customs or railhead town fewer and more pleasure boats and sailboats; the share of the core's vacant storefronts is also the share of empty slips and the weight of derelicts (a few degrees' list, faded, gear gone) and of one that has sunk at its mooring. Boats (`{ id, kind, x, z, heading, length, beam, draft, state, colour, name, mooring }`; troller, gillnetter, crabber, tug, skiff, sailboat, pleasure; 4.5 to 20 m) lie in slips, alongside piers, a few at anchor, and a tug by the terminal's barge. They do not move.
- **Pilings.** Fields of dead piles (uneven tops, some leaning, gaps) where a closed mill's or a cannery's wharf was pulled down (or an old fish dock), with the year in a sentence of the history's list; dolphins (three piles lashed) off the longer pier ends.
- **The mill's water.** A working mill has a log pond held by a boom of sticks chained between piles, floating logs, and a jack ladder to a jack house; a closed mill's boom is empty and, if the mill is old enough and not cleared, the rusting wigwam burner stands on its site.
- **The terminal.** A yard by what the port handles (decks of logs in trapezoid stacks and perhaps a chip pile, silos and a bulk shed, rows of containers, rows of cars), on the terminal's open lots (the terminal's blocks are built to 42 per cent so there is room), and at the port's wharf (the wharf street's pier, in the cove's deep water) a loaded barge with its tug and, for grain and chips, a loading spout. A terminal the history says is idle (more likely the emptier the town) has an empty yard and no barge.
- **Lights.** A white harbour light on one headland's tip and a day beacon on the other (the tongues are the samples where the site's shore runs far seaward), and a light at the end of the longest pier. Structures only: no lamps are lit.

Depth is not read from the ground (the plan reads the site alone): it is estimated from the distance seaward of the shore, one way in the cove between its headlands and a shallower one outside, and checked in `test/town/harbour.test.ts` against the coast's real floor, which finds about 2 per cent of boat footprint points over a sea stack the site cannot see.

### The ground the game lays over the coast

### How the town is drawn

`client/src/game/town/` turns the plan and the ground's height into plain arrays (positions, normals, vertex colours,
indices), with no renderer, so the builders are tested as pure functions:

- **Streets and junctions** (`streetGeometry.ts`, `junctionGeometry.ts`, `junctionPaint.ts`, `signGeometry.ts`, `surface.ts`, `junctionWorld.ts`, `signFont.ts`). Every surface is the walked ground's height (which is already cut down for the roadway: see "The ground the game lays over the coast") plus a lift of a centimetre or so: a roadway 1.2 cm, the sidewalk's slabs 1.2 cm with up to 1.6 cm of heave at a joint (never more than 3 cm over the lot's ground), a curb's top at the lots' level (read 30 cm past the curb line) over a face down to the gutter, a corner sidewalk 2.8 cm; a vertex follows the ground's roll (a junction's cross-slopes are resolved by this: its pavement is draped over the ground as a street's strips are, with no plane of its own). A street is drawn by what its side has (`plan.edges`): the roadway in its paving stretches (the new dark asphalt after 2000 where under half worn, else the old, cracked one, each with a tone of its own, the texture running along the street, a seam across between stretches), the curb in sections of about 3 m (some replaced, lighter), the sidewalk slab by slab (`walkGeometry.ts`: tooled joints every 1.35 to 1.8 m by the pour, each pour's tone by its decade, slab by slab heave at the joints, cracks, broken corners, slabs gone and filled with asphalt or gravel, dirt and moss thickening toward the back edge and in the joints, weeds in the joints and over the edge, a back edge that wanders by up to 18 cm, a rusted cover or two), and where a side has no curb an asphalt edge that wanders by 10 to 20 cm against a gravel shoulder with its own ragged edge. `roadWear.ts` lays utility cuts (darker rectangles at angles, some overlapping, with sealed seams), sealed cracks, potholes on the least-used streets and the worst paving, the gutter's band of needles and dirt, gravel dragged out of alley mouths, manholes and storm drains; the paint is worn (some dashes short, some gone, the rest faded by the street's use). `yardGeometry.ts` lays each lot's drive (gravel bed, dirt two-track, concrete ribbon), front walk, bare and mossy patches, and the worn verge (a drive's apron, a desire line from the curb to the sidewalk, a pull-off for a bare yard). An alley is two wheel tracks of gravel with grass between. A street is cut where it meets another: its roadway strip ends at each junction's `mouth`, its verge, sidewalk and curb at the corner's tangent point, and the pavement between is the junction's own, ONE surface bounded by its curb returns (an ear-cut polygon split in four, once or twice, so no triangle is much over 9 m), drawn first; the strips of roadway laid after it are laid without any ground already laid (`RoadSet`: a convex tile minus the convex pieces it overlaps), so no two roadway surfaces overlap anywhere. The corner is a curb along the return and the sidewalk behind it in one piece to the two lot lines; a ramp is cut through the curb at each side of each leg's crosswalk (where both sides have a curb and a sidewalk), 4 ft wide, laid in strips across so it follows the ground's own cut (the ramp's run is the ground's 30 cm, not the research's 1.81 m: the walking and the drawing agree, which a smooth ramp would not), with a 2 ft yellow dome panel at its foot; a ramp-less corner has a full-height curb round, and a corner where a leg has no curb is a plain asphalt radius with a gravel shoulder. The paint is the research's: ladder bars 2 ft wide with gaps of 5 ft at most, 10 ft long, just outside the pavement on each marked leg; a stop line 18 in wide 4 ft before the crosswalk (6 ft from the pavement where none is marked); the main street's centre line in 10 ft dashes with 30 ft gaps ending 5 ft short of a crosswalk and carried through the junction as a dotted line of 2 ft dashes with 6 ft gaps; parking stalls held back 20 ft from a crosswalk and 30 ft from a STOP sign; the avenue's median ending short of the junction; none at all on a residential junction or its streets. Signs are geometry: a STOP sign (a red octagon 30 in across on a post 1 ft behind the curb, its bottom 7 ft over the curb's top, "STOP" in white), green street-name blades on a post at one corner (a blade for each street, parallel to it, the plan's name in capitals, as geometry from a 5 by 7 pixel face, `signFont.ts`, on both faces on the full tier and blank on the low), a DEAD END sign, a PAVEMENT ENDS diamond, three red diamond markers 18 in on a side 4 ft over the road at a turnaround. A T's through street runs unbroken across its top; an alley's mouth is a flared driveway apron through the verge with the curb a lip, the sidewalk carried straight across at its own level and the alley's gravel rising to it. The wharf is a timber deck; ground under the sea is not drawn; a surface that reaches into the highway's bed is cut along its edge, the bed being the highway's own paving (next).
- **The highway's paving** (`highwayGeometry.ts`, `highwayBed.ts`). The engine shapes a flat bed 11 m wide for the highway and paints 7 m of asphalt on it with a gravel shoulder either side, so a street that ended at the bed's edge would meet gravel and another asphalt. Where the highway runs through the town the town paves the whole bed itself, in its own street asphalt, in stretches of 40 to 70 m (`highwayLook.ts`: each with its own layer, tone and age, and a texture frame of its own turned to the road's direction at its middle, so the grain and the cracks run along the road wherever it bends; a seam crosses it between stretches), laid in bands across so that dirt and wear gather toward the edges (and cut across again, near the town's streets, until each band's plane follows the walked ground: the trough of a street that meets the highway rises through the bed), at the ground's height and a roadway's lift, as one more roadway in the flat layer: so the streets and the highway are one surface, in the same cells and batches as the streets (no mesh of its own). The bed is cut into tiles of 2 m along z, each a quad between two chords of the centre line (`highwayBed.ts`); every surface of the town is cut off those tiles (`Surface`), and the paving is laid on them, so the two share their edges exactly however the centre line bends. A junction's pavement is solved against the highway's edge as a straight line, which lies up to a quarter of a metre in or out of the bed's edge: where it lies in, the pavement is cut off the tiles; where it lies out, the junction takes in the ground between the edge and the bed (`bedFillers`) as its own pavement, so there is no gap and no face is drawn twice at a meeting; the curb returns end where they did. The strip runs from 25 m (measured along the centre line) before the first street that meets the highway to 25 m after the last, and each end is a clean line square to the highway, as a street's made end is: beyond it the stream below takes over. It has the wear a street has, drawn from a stream of its own by slots of 32 m (`layFeatures`: patches, utility cuts with sealed seams, sealed cracks, potholes, standing water, wheel tracks and oil in both lanes, stains, and a manhole every 90 m or so; no drains, it has no curb), none of it in a slot where a street's trough reaches into the bed. Its marks are in the streets' style and rank: a double yellow centre line, broken across the roadway of each street that meets the highway, and a white line at each lane's edge (3.5 m from the centre, where the engine's asphalt ends), broken across the opening of each pavement on its side; the highway is kept up, so its paint is faded by a roll of its own per 8 m and dirtied, none of it worn through. The highway has no stop line of its own, and the side streets keep theirs; where the plan marks a crosswalk across the highway (`marked` on its edge leg, at the junctions of the core) it is laid on this paving as a ladder of bars across the bed, in the size of any crosswalk's, at the lift and rank of the streets' marks, with the centre line and both lane lines broken across it. The pavement of a junction may meet the edge in more than one stretch (two streets that meet it close together, with a corner of lot between them), and each is a meeting of its own. Where the town's spur crosses the highway, the paving and its marks are cut off the track's bed (its whole width), which keeps its own ground. Nothing grows on the bed (the weeds of a sidewalk's joints, `growth.ts`, stand off it) and a made end's gravel is cut off it (`endsGeometry.ts`). Both tiers have the strip and every mark.
- **The highway outside the strip** (`highwayCells.ts`, `highwayParts.ts`, with the look of `highwayLook.ts` that the strip shares). The engine's road is endless (`roadCenterX` and `roadGradeAt` are pure functions of z), so past the strip the game draws it as cells of 128 m along the road, built as the player comes near and let go as they leave, as the rail's are (`railParts.ts`): a cell is a pure function of the seed, its index and a level, any machine builds the same one in any order, and nothing of the plan is read beyond the strip's own two ends, so a cell builds at any z. They are built within 600 m of the camera (300 on the low tier) and let go past 720 (380): the rail's 4300 m is for a ballast strip of a few triangles a metre, this road is about 20 vertices a metre with its wear, and beyond 600 m the engine's own painted road shows, as before. Within 200 m (100 low) a cell is the near one (rows of 2 m, the bed's own tiles, and all the wear); beyond, the far one (rows of 8 m and the large patches only), with 40 m of room before a level changes; at most three cell jobs are begun at once, nearest first, and a job yields between slots. Each cell is one mesh in the rail's flat material (the town's, with the flat layer's depth lead and its draw order), so it is outside the town's 170-mesh count, as the rail's are: at 600 m the lead is about 50 m of depth (a step is z * z / (0.05 * 2^24) m), the same as the rail's cells accept at their distance. The cross-section out of town is a rural two-lane road: asphalt 7 m wide (the width the engine paints) with a ragged edge that wanders by a decimetre or two (the street's own `edgeOffset`), gravel shoulders (the town's gravel layer, at the street shoulder's rank and group) to the bed's edge at 5.5 m, a white edge line 0.4 m inside the asphalt's edge (3.1 m from the centre) and a single yellow centre line of dashes 3 m long in every 12 m (in z), in the streets' worn paint. At the strip's end the asphalt keeps its whole width to the end line's farther corner, then narrows from 11 m to 7 m over 10 m (the edge line moves from 3.5 m to 3.1 m with it), and the double yellow gives way to the dashes at the end line. What lies between the strip's two end lines is the strip's: each polygon of a cell is cut to what lies before the start line or past the end line, a cell wholly inside is not built, and the soft bands of the wheel tracks are laid only where all their corners lie in it. Where the town's spur comes within the bed its ground is left out, as the strip leaves it (the main line runs 16 m seaward of the centre line and never meets the highway; the engine has no bridge or trestle on the road). Everything is a function of the world's seed and the place along the road: stretches by a jittered boundary every 55 m (set on the 2 m grid), features by slots of 32 m that each lie in one cell (a feature lies wholly in its slot, so none is cut by a cell's edge and none lies over another cell's), the stream's key `highwayKey(seed)`, nothing of the plan's streams, so `TOWN_GEN` is untouched. Heights are read once for a z on the centre line (the engine's ground is exactly level across the bed: `highwayStream.test.ts` holds it) and the street trough where it is (`streetLiftD`). A cell takes about 3 to 4 ms to make on a loaded laptop (the near one), 20 vertices and 11 triangles a metre, 1.1 KB a metre on the GPU; the 1200 m standing at 600 m is about 1.4 MB. `__capeHighway.stats()` in the page's console gives what stands.
- **Made ends** (`endsGeometry.ts`): the pavement stops at a clean line and gravel runs beyond for 6 m, a bulb for a turnaround, a barricade of two striped boards on three posts, a timber bumper at a landing.
- **Where a house meets the ground** (`houseSeam.ts`, `seamWall.ts`, `seamGround.ts`, and the growth at the walls in `growth.ts`). The house builder notes each house's walls, foundation, door and steps or porch; the passes after it dress the seam by the household's record (the yard's upkeep, the house's era and state): a foundation in dull concrete, block or brick with splash, algae and uneven skirt boards; ragged patches of soil, moss, gravel or bark along the walls, wider at a downspout; things at the door and against the walls; patches of clover, moss, straw and bare earth on the lawn; ferns, dock and bramble at the walls of a house nobody keeps. The grass itself is the engine's, let up to the wall by the clearing.
- **Paved ground** (`groundGeometry.ts`): on each block of a paved district a patch 4.8 cm over the ground in 8 m tiles (a concrete walk in the core, gravel on the dock end, the mill's site and industrial flats, asphalt on the waterfront and the highway strip, which has a row of painted stalls where a whole stall fits clear of the buildings). The paved polygons are one list the simulation side owns (`sim/town/pavedGround.ts`, cached per plan): those blocks, and the APRON of each gas station, motel and fire hall that stands alone beyond the last blocks (the lot inset 2.5 m at its sides and back and 0.5 m at the street, and no deeper than 3 m behind the building: asphalt, with the unpaved margin left to weeds). The drawing lays its patches by that list, and the clearing (`clearing.ts`) shuts trees and every kind of ground cover over each polygon and a margin beyond it (a block's `BLOCK_MARGIN`, an apron's `APRON_MARGIN`), and the streets' field (`streetField.ts`) cuts the terrain under it, so the three cannot disagree about where there is paving. The lot's drive, walk and patches are not laid on an apron.
- **Roads out** (`roadGeometry.ts`): strips by surface (asphalt, gravel, two wheel tracks) narrowing along the road, and each destination drawn crudely: a cemetery's fence and headstones, a landing's logs, a pit, a tank on legs, a gate.
- **Surfaces** (`townSurface.ts`, `groundSurfaces.ts`, `buildingSurfaces.ts`): see "The town's textures", below. Streets and ground wear asphalt, sidewalk concrete and gravel; buildings their walls' and roofs' layers.
- **Buildings** (`buildingGeometry.ts` and the files it calls; see "Buildings from the plan", below): shells on the plan's oriented rectangles (on sloping ground the floor is the highest ground under the footprint and the walls run down to the lowest; a front on a slope is entered at the sidewalk's height), roofs by kind (flat with a parapet, gable, hip, shed; the oldest wooden gables sag at the ridge), walls cut around their openings, fronts, rooms, windows, signs and parts from the plan's `uses`, vacant buildings dull and boarded, ruins roofless and broken at the top, and one touch for each landmark (a cornice, a flagpole and portico for the municipal building, a steeple, an open tower on legs for the refuge, a mill's stack). Colour comes from the material and the building's id; no random call is made.
- **The quay** (`quayGeometry.ts`): along each wet stretch an apron (a deck) and a straight wall a hand's width beyond its edge, so the bank is hidden: timber cribwork under a timber cap, or, where a fire after 1921 took the waterfront, steel sheet piling under a concrete cap (`plan.quay.wall`), with wales or a corrugated face, fender piles every 6 m, ladders every 40 m and bollards every 15 m.
- **Decks and rails** (`pileGeometry.ts`): each deck of `plan.decks` with its top at exactly the height the walking uses, a skirt, and piles for piers and sheds; a timber bull rail with posts along every edge the simulation holds a walker back from.
- **Parked vehicles as models** (full tier; `tools/models/build.py`, `client/assets/models/`, `game/town/props/vehicle*.ts`). Twelve files (`vehicle.<id>.glb`, 11 MB in all) and a manifest (`vehicles.json`: footprint, wheels' contact points, roles) are written from the downloaded sources: each baked to one frame (metres, +x the front, on y = 0 at the tyres), joined into one mesh a role (`paint`, `glass`, `lamp`, `plate`, `tyre`, `under`, `interior`, or `body` for the worn ones), the paint a colour with no texture. The plan gives each vehicle its model (`sim/town/port/props/vehicleModels.ts`, from its kind, state, load, age and seed; a camper has none) and its box is the model's footprint, so the walker's box is the file's own; the room planned for a kind in `tuning.ts` is the largest of its models. `vehicleFleet.ts` stands each on the plane fitted through its four wheels' ground contacts, `vehicleLook.ts` gives it a paint from a table of dull period colours, grime, dust and (for derelicts and project cars) algae, `vehicleMaterial.ts` wears that by instance, and `vehicleParts.ts` loads the files once the town's geometry is made, one patient job a model, and draws a model's roles as thin instances culled each frame to the view and 160 m. The contact patch under each stays in the town's flat layer; a model that fails to load is left out. The low tier, campers and trailers stay code-built. The engine's loader hands every model back mirrored in x; the car that is driven has its parts turned half a circle about up to answer that (see "The car"), and the parked vehicles' parts are not turned.
- **Props** (`game/town/props/`, drawn after the harbour, as a generator of slices): vehicles are lofted through stations from the rear bumper to the nose (a smooth-shaded `MeshBuilder.grid`; the wheel arches cut by raising the lower edge of the stations over each wheel; side glass and screens as the town's see-through effect over a dark cabin with seats you can make out; wheels in the arches; bumpers, lamps, mirrors, door seams), in the roof-bitumen layer (the smoothest of the town's layers) tinted by the paint, with rust at the arches and sills of old ones, grime low on the body, a faded roof and bonnet, and algae at the seals of one that has not moved; a work vehicle carries its gear, a project car stands on blocks under a tarp, a derelict sits low on flat tyres. Boats are lofted hulls on trailers (aluminium skiff in the sheet-metal layer, fibreglass runabout), some under a tarp, some with weeds through the trailer. Poles are tapered timbers of the weathered-boards layer; wires are three-sided tubes wider than a real wire, hung in a parabola. Fences follow the ground post by post; chain-link is a faint see-through sheet with rails and posts, because a mesh of wires would shimmer. Each vehicle has a ragged dark stain of ground under it and, beyond it, a soft dark that fades out past its outline (see "The sky a wall sees"; the scene has no shadow). The low tier lofts through the shape's own stations, draws three vehicles in five with no interior, mirrors or seams, thinner poles and wires, no service drops, and fewer fences and gear.
- **Boats and the harbour's things** (`boatGeometry.ts`, `harbourGeometry.ts`): hulls lofted through six stations with a sheer line, wheelhouses with dark windows and the kind's gear; piles, boom sticks and logs, the jack ladder, the burner, the yard's stacks, silos, containers and cars, the barge, and the lights.
- **Batches** (`townGeometry.ts`): by cells of 128 m, one set of arrays for what lies flat and one for what stands, built as a generator of slices the engine's scheduler spreads over frames, so no frame stalls on the town.

`townParts.ts` is the renderer part: a `RenderGame` that wraps the engine's `noParts` and, once the scene is registered, makes
a Lite mesh of each batch as a job of the scheduler, the cells nearest the start first, under the town's material (`townMaterial.ts`), the engine's air and wet look as
its props are, with the flat layer drawn a depth step ahead of the terrain (toward the eye: the renderer's depth runs from 1 at the near plane to 0 far away, so the layer's steps and a rank's share are added to the clip depth, and `test/townWorld/depthLead.test.ts` holds their signs; with the other sign a surface with no cut under it, a yard's drive or walk, is covered by the terrain from a few metres off, and a mark by the surface it lies on. The layer takes no share of the engine's slope-scaled offset: that is worked out a vertex at a time, so two surfaces at one place got different shares wherever their triangles differ, by far more than a rank's step, and a patch was eaten by the roadway under it in a wedge that moved with the eye. A surface over ground the terrain is not cut under, a shoulder's outer edge or an alley's wheel tracks, lies `OPEN_GROUND_LIFT` (4 cm) over it, as a yard's surfaces do, since the terrain is drawn a few centimetres over the ground wherever it bends). Lite culls no mesh, so each frame shows a cell's
meshes while its bounding sphere is in the view's planes. The standing meshes are registered as hard cover for the rain (on
the tiers above low). The low tier builds simpler rooms, windows and trim (see "Buildings from the plan"); the
geometry is otherwise the same on all three tiers. The glass and the signs are meshes of their own, drawn blended after the opaque ones. One thing to know of Lite: its PBR pipelines are built when the scene is
registered, and know vertex colours only if a mesh of the scene had them then, so `townParts` stands one hidden triangle of no
size with a colour a vertex in the scene from the start (`early`), as the engine's lake panorama does. The town's textured materials need nothing of the seed: their pipelines (the UV, the plugin's vertex data and its two texture arrays) are made when the first mesh of one joins the registered scene, as the engine's props' plugin pipelines are.

### The town's textures

The town's geometry wears scanned CC0 PBR textures (each work credited in `CREDITS.md`) on its walls, roofs and street surfaces. Everything else is still flat vertex colour.

- **The files.** `tools/textures/build.py` turns the sources into `client/assets/textures/` (Git LFS): for each of 22 layers, `town.<name>.color.webp` (512 x 512, sRGB; a layer that is *paintable* (painted lap siding, stucco, aged corrugated metal and the generated asphalt shingles) is neutral grey detail, luminance over its mean times 0.5, for a paint colour to multiply) and `town.<name>.data.webp` (512 x 512 lossless RGBA, data: R, G the normal's x and y in the OpenGL convention, B roughness, A = 0.5 + 0.5 AO; AO is in the upper half of alpha because a browser that decodes through a canvas premultiplies). `town.layers.json` lists each layer: name, index, asset id, credit id (the work's section in `CREDITS.md`, which alone says where it came from), tile size in metres across and up (the figures the source gives, and the project's estimates, marked `tileEstimated`), paintable, generated. The layers are walls (siding_painted, planks_peeling, shingle_siding, planks_weathered, brick, metal_aged, metal_painted, concrete_board, stucco, stone, plywood, shutter), roofs (roof_metal, roof_bitumen, roof_shingle) and ground (sidewalk, asphalt, asphalt_dark, gravel, mud, moss, rust). `asphalt_dark` (new paving, a cut, a seam) and `mud` (grime: dirt in joints and gutters, worn ground) are used by the worn ground and the buildings; `moss` and `rust` are the wear overlays. The game registers its files beside the engine's (`assets.ts`, `mergeAssets`), by their names under `textures/`.
- **The builders' interface.** `MeshBuilder.poly(points, hint, colour, surface?)` (also `quad` and `wall`): a `Surface` is a layer name, a `UvFrame` in metres (an origin and two axes on the polygon's plane; each vertex's UV is its offset along each axis divided by the layer's tile size, so a texture is at true scale; swapping the axes turns it on its side), an optional `tint` (what the texture is multiplied by: by default the polygon's colour for a paintable layer and white for the rest) and a `wear` (a share 0 to 1 of the surface, a number or a function of a vertex's position) with a `wearKind` (`moss`, the default, `rust`, `peel`, `algae` or `grime`), whether it runs in `streaks`, and a second wear (`wear2`, `wearKind2`, `streaks2`) laid over the first. `colour` stays the flat colour the polygon is drawn in where there is no texture and until the textures land. `wallFrame`, `edgeFrame` and `groundFrame` make the usual frames. The arrays gain `uvs` and `surface` (twelve floats a vertex: layer or -1, the first wear's amount, its kind, the glow; tint rgb, how clear it is; the second wear's amount and kind, two spare). A polygon with no surface is unchanged.
- **The material** (`townMaterial.ts`, a Lite `MaterialPlugin`, priority 200). Per fragment, at `CUSTOM_FRAGMENT_UPDATE_DIFFUSE`: it samples the layer's colour and data arrays by the UV, multiplies the colour by the tint, sets roughness, occlusion and the normal (a tangent frame from screen-space derivatives of the world position and the UV: no tangent attribute), and lays two wears' overlays (moss, rust, peeling boards, algae film, grime: each kind's layer and colour grade in the plugin) where three octaves of world-space value noise are under the wear's amount, with a soft edge: patches, or, for streaks, noise stretched down the surface. Bare metal sheet is half metallic and the wear takes that away. A surface can also light itself (`glow`) and be see-through (`clear`, with the reflection by direction and the grime described under windows and glass), which is how a room's walls (baked light in the vertex tint), a lit sign and the glass work; the sign layer's plugin also reads the lettering atlas. A vertex with no layer is untouched. The vertex packing and the injection points are in the file's header. The colour array is an sRGB texture (filtering in linear light), the data array is data; both have mips and repeat. Lite takes no anisotropy.
- **Loading** (`townTextures.ts`). The arrays are made empty with the renderer's part and filled in place by one upload when the 44 images have been decoded (four at a time, the engine's decoder, `decodeGroundLayer`); until then the plugin's `townReady` uniform is 0 and the town draws as flat colour. The part's `waiting()` counts the images. If the arrays cannot be made the town stays flat colour, with one warning. On the low tier the arrays are 256 x 256 (a quarter of the memory), the wear has two octaves and its overlay brings colour and roughness but no second normal. The full tiers' arrays are about 62 MB of GPU memory with their mips, the low tier's 15 MB.
- **What is textured.** Walls by the plan's material (`buildingSurfaces.ts`: wood is painted lap siding in the house's colour, one in four in shingle siding, sheds and the sheds on piles half in weathered boards, some vacant houses in peeling boards; brick, concrete, stone, stucco and metal to their layers), with the courses running along the wall from the building's base and the four walls end to end round the building; roofs by kind (pitched: shingles with their courses along the eave; shed and metal: metal roofing with its ribs down the slope; flat: bitumen); boarded openings in plywood and big doors in the shutter layer; roadways and junction pavements in asphalt, sidewalks, curbs, ramps and the blocks' concrete walks in the sidewalk concrete, alleys and gravel yards and road ends in gravel, by the flat colour they are laid in (`groundSurfaces.ts`), with the world's x and z as their UVs. Wear is a share of the surface: a little on every wall, more on vacant buildings and ruins, more at a wall's foot; on the ground, dirt (the `grime` kind, in the second wear slot, over moss in the first) and moss by cause (see "How the town is drawn": sidewalks, roadways and yards).
- **Short-lived memory.** The terrain's functions used to answer with small objects (a value and its slopes), and every reading of the ground made hundreds: a load made about 13 GB of them in its first half minute, and a page at rest about 50 MB a second, which is what swells the heap between collections. They now leave their answers in registers: one record with fixed fields that lives as long as its module and is written in place, read into locals at once after the call (anything may write it again: the same helper is called many times, and the engine's coast and the town's ground ask each other). A register is a record and never a module's `let`, because a `let` holding a double is boxed again at each write. The engine's noise, coast, massif and rock layer do this from v0.2.13, and the town's ground (`sim/town/`: `fill.ts`, `cover.ts`, `streetField.ts`, `ground.ts`, `shoreSpline.ts`, `clearing.ts`, `railGround.ts`, and `place.ts`'s `shapeD`, which makes the one record the engine is owed) with it; exported functions keep their signatures as wrappers. A load makes about 4.9 GB where it made 11.5, and a page at rest about 9 MB a second. The numbers are the same to the bit: `test/townGround/groundDigest.test.ts` and `coverDigest.test.ts` hold digests taken from the code before the change, and a digest that moves means arithmetic moved. Earlier, the engine's wildlife director was given a lattice of kept heights for its lines of sight (v0.2.12). What is left is the numbers a call passes (a double handed to a function that is not inlined is boxed), the mesh builders' own points, and one record at each crossing between the engine and the place.
- **Model textures are KTX2.** The vehicles' images (`client/assets/models/`) and, from v0.2.10, the engine's own models' are KTX2, read through `KHR_texture_basisu` and transcoded to the GPU's block format at one byte a texel; as WebP, JPEG and PNG they decoded to four (about 470 MB of GPU memory between them, now about 120 MB). `tools/models/test/manifest.test.mjs` holds the vehicles to it. The town's own texture arrays and the sign atlas are still four bytes a texel.
- **A builder's vertices are 32-bit numbers from the start** (`meshBuilder.ts`, `Run`). A builder kept its positions, normals, colours, UVs, surface records and indices in plain arrays, eight bytes a number, and copied them into typed arrays at the end: while a town was built the tab's JS heap stood at 1.4 GB. Each is now a typed array that doubles as it fills, and `townGeometry` takes each cell's arrays out of its builder (`take`), which lets the builder's storage go. The heap's peak while a town loads is about 640 MB.
- **A mesh's arrays are let go once the GPU has them** (`leanMesh.ts`). Lite keeps what a mesh was made from and its plugin's per-vertex data for as long as the mesh lives, which held every vertex of the town twice (96 bytes in the tab and 96 on the GPU, about 200 MB of the tab's memory). The game reads none of it back, so `townParts.ts` and `railParts.ts` drop each mesh's arrays as soon as its buffers are written (the plugin's is written by the first frame that reaches the mesh).
- **A vertex's surface record is 16 bytes** (`surfacePack.ts`, `townMaterial.ts`). The twelve numbers `MeshBuilder` writes a vertex are kept on the GPU as two 16-bit floats (the hours or a pane's tilt, and the sky or the rank: as they were, so they come back as they went) and three 32-bit words of bit fields: the tint's three numbers as their square roots in 12 bits each (1024 steps to a tint of 1, so 1 is exact, the largest is 16, and a tint is within 0.05 percent at 1); the first wear's amount and how clear (8 bits, 1/255); the glow (50 steps to 1, from -1); the layer (its index plus one, 5 bits) and the first wear's kind (4 bits of whole number and 7 of fraction in 126ths, so a half is exact and a pane's seed is within 1/252); and the second wear's amount (10 bits, 256 to 1) and kind (10 bits, 64 to 1: wholes and halves exact), which in a pane are its height and width. Four plugin attributes carry them (`float16x2` and three `uint32`, formats every WebGPU browser has) and the top of the vertex stage (`TOWN_UNPACK`) unpacks them into the names the rest of the stage has always read, so no shader code below it moved. It was 24 bytes (twelve 16-bit floats); with 2.15 million vertices that is 52 MB less 17. Ten bits of tint were tried first and moved the low tier's pictures by 0.4 of a level, all of it the tint. `test/townWorld/surfacePack.test.ts` holds each float's bound over two tiers of a whole town.
- **A vertex's normal and colour are smaller, and a small batch's indices are 16-bit** (`vertexPack.ts`, `createPackedMesh`; the renderer's `createMeshFromData` takes the formats). A town mesh's normal was three 32-bit floats and its colour four; the normal is now `snorm16x4` (four 16-bit values, the fourth 0: a unit normal is turned by at most 0.0015 degrees) and the colour `float16x4` (within 0.0005 of any value the town writes: the tint runs to 1.571 and the alpha carries codes, so 8 bits would not do for it). A vertex is 52 bytes with its surface record (position 12, uv 8, normal 8, colour 8, record 16) where it was 64, and a batch of fewer than 65,536 vertices (150 of the full tier's 155 batches, all 125 of the low tier's) has 2-byte indices where it had 4: on the medium tier at 1280 x 720 the town's geometry buffers fell from 118.0 to 87.6 MB. `snorm8x4` was tried first (`NORMAL_FORMAT`, one constant, the sizes follow): the low tier's picture of wet ground at the quay moved by 0.4 of a level on average (the unchanged build moves 0.15 of one between two runs there) where the 16-bit normal moves nothing past that. The town, the railway, the highway's cells and the rooms are made this way; the parked vehicles, the car and the marks are not (they come from models or carry thin instances and are small). A mesh made this way is never written in place or resized (the renderer refuses), and the arrays it keeps (`_cpuPacked`) are let go with the rest (`leanMesh.ts`). `test/townWorld/vertexPack.test.ts` holds the bounds over two tiers of a whole town.
- **A stand-in car is released when its model lands** (`townParts.ts`, `retireStandIns`). The code-built car that stands for a parked vehicle until its model is made (`props/vehicleStandIn.ts`) was only hidden when the model landed: its mesh and buffers stayed for good (3.6 MB in the town of "sorrow"). It is now taken out of the scene, its buffers released and its entry out of the list walked every frame; the stand-in of a model whose file fails stays, as it is drawn for good. What is left when the game ends is released once.
- **The ground's heights are remembered, but not without end** (`townGeometry.ts`, `heightMemo`). `townGround`'s `heightAt` keeps the height of each point to the millimetre in a table; the build asks for 720 thousand readings of 375 thousand points, nearly every repeat close behind the first, so the table holds at most `HEIGHT_MEMO_CAP` (32768) points and is emptied when full, which costs the build 0.6 percent more readings. After the build the walkers, the cars and the camera ask for new points every frame, and the table that kept them all grew by about 1,400 points a second, 69 bytes each. The height is a pure function of the point, so a forgotten point is read again as the same number.
- **Nothing is a flat colour: materials** (`materials.ts`). A polygon with no `Surface` of its own takes a material: a name (`"wood"`, `"boards"`, `"painted"`, `"steel"`, `"rusty"`, `"concrete"`, `"brick"`, `"cloth"`, `"plastic"`, `"rubber"` and the rest of the table) that stands for a layer and how it is laid. The frame is found from the polygon (level faces on the world's x and z, standing ones along themselves and up, a board's grain along its longest edge), and the builder's colour is kept: a paintable layer is painted it, any other layer is tinted to it over the layer's own mean colour. A builder names the material on the polygon (in the place of a surface), or round a block of code (`withMat`), or on a smooth grid (`GridLook.mat`). A polygon nobody named takes `"painted"` and is counted (`unnamed`); `client/test/townWorld/materials.test.ts` holds the count at zero and finds no opaque vertex without a layer in either tier. Only glass, wires and the sign atlas's faces carry no layer. The game has no layer for plastic, rubber, cloth, rope, galvanised sheet or smooth painted metal: those materials are stand-ins on the nearest layer (the fine grain of the sidewalk's concrete, the dark asphalt, the rust-and-paint layer), and are the first things a new layer would replace.
- **Doors** (`buildingKit.ts`: `doorIn`, `pairedDoors`, `glazed`). A door's leaf fills its hole from edge to edge and the hole's reveal runs exactly to the leaf, wherever the ledger put it, so nothing is seen round a door or past it. A house's or a hall's door carries its panel and its light on the leaf (glass over a curtain or the dark of the hall); a shop's is a kick panel under glass in a frame, with a transom or a rail to the head, so the room is seen through the glass alone. Nothing lies across a door: a house's foundation stops at its steps or porch, and a shop front's water table stops at every door. **A shop's door opens for the people who walk through it** (`openDoors.ts`, pure; `frontGeometry.ts`, `townMaterial.ts`). The door of every open unit has a leaf with a thickness (4.5 cm), an inner face, edges, its glass in two panes (one looking in) and a pull and a push plate, all tagged as the leaf's by the alpha of their corners' colour (`MeshBuilder.withLeaf`, `DOOR_ALPHA` 0.02, under every paint rank's); a pair of doors (an opening 1.1 m or wider) is two leaves, of which the right-hand one as you go in opens. Which door and when is the walkers' list alone (`doorsFor`): each unit a trip begins or ends at has a day of keyframes made by following its walkers in time order, and `angleAt(door, hour)` reads it, so a leaf's angle is a function of the hour with nothing kept, a second walker finds it still open or opens it again from where it is with no jump, and the door and the walker use one hour (`peopleParts.hourNow`). The leaf turns about its hinge edge into the room (80 to 95 degrees, opening 0.5 to 0.7 s, closing 1.4 to 2.2 s, eased both ends, the hinge on either side by a roll on the unit, the pull on the other); it begins to open as the walker (who begins 1.8 to 2 m inside the leaf's plane, clear of its sweep) begins, or when an arriving walker is 1.4 m short of it, and is let go 0.7 m after they pass. The vertex stage does the turning: the ten doors nearest the eye that are not shut ride in the town material's uniforms (`townDoors`, `doorA<i>`, `doorB<i>`, vertex-visible), and at the world matrix (`DOOR_VERTEX`) a vertex whose colour's alpha says leaf takes the turn of the moving door whose shut leaf it stands in, so its normal turns with it in every material of the town and a door that does not move costs nothing. A walker inside the room with the door shut is not drawn (`hiddenByDoor`), so they appear as the opening door shows them and are gone when it has shut behind them; a room with less than 1.8 m of clear floor beyond its door has no walkers. **An open doorway shows its room as the window does, with no glass** (`PaneLook.open`, `OPEN_SEED` and `OPEN_BEHIND` in `buildingKit.ts`; `frontGeometry.ts`; `townAlphaCode`): where the rooms are read through their glass (see "Rooms"), the opening of every door whose leaf swings has a pane of its own, fixed to the doorway (not tagged as the leaf's: it does not turn) and standing 5 cm behind the shut leaf's inner face, behind its pull's plate, so the shut leaf, its glass, its lettering plate and its handle are all in front of it and it is hidden by depth. It is a marked pane (the `through` layer, opaque, written after the break) with a seed of 6 to 7 where a glazed pane's is 4 to 5, and the glass's code makes it nothing of its own (alpha 0, no colour: no film, dirt, streaks or reflection), so it is the room as the blur reads it, out of focus by how far behind the doorway each place is (sharp at the threshold, soft from 0.25 m, whole by 1.3 m), with the same glow and haze, and it hides the lines behind it by how soft it read them, so the ink line fades the same way and is gone where the blur is whole. Because it writes depth, what is behind the doorway's plane is read through it and what is in front is not: the leaf as it swings into the room goes behind the pane (its own glass is a marked pane behind this one and loses to it by depth), a walker going in is sharp at the threshold and softens as they go deeper (and sharpens coming out), and a walker, a prop or a person on the street side is in front and untouched. The notice on a shop's door (its hours plate, in the lettering layer) is not the leaf's and does not swing: as before, it stays where it was hung, in front of the pane, so with the leaf open it hangs in the doorway over the soft room, sharp. Where there is no kept frame (the sheet path) the pane is not laid and the doorway is as it was. The low tier draws its doors shut (the leaf, its swing and these panes are the full tier's), so nothing of this is on it. The door does not open for the player, and nobody reaches for a handle: a player goes in at the door behind a dark screen to a copy of the room built again 60 m over the street (`sim/inside/rooms.ts`), and the town's building stays a solid to everyone, and the player on foot is held out of the building's hull, so the eye is never behind a doorway's pane (only a debugging free camera could be).

### People in the shops

The open shops have people doing what people do there (`game/town/people.ts`, `peopleParts.ts`; five models, `client/assets/models/person.<name>.glb`, each with 21 clips and three primitives, listed by hand in `people.json`; the files hold the clips two and a half times too fast and a body plays them at two fifths of its rate (`CLIP_STORED`), so the table of bone matrices the clips are baked into holds twenty-four rows to a second of movement and is no larger than it was with twelve clips). They are the drawing's, not the simulation's: no part of the plan, drawn from a hash of the building's id, the unit's id and the person's index, so the same plan gives the same people on every machine, and `TOWN_GEN` does not move. Only a unit that is open has any, and they are in the front of the room, within `FRONT_ZONE` (about 2 m) of the glass, in front of the room's painted sheet (`roomBackdrop.ts`), which is the part of the room that is drawn and seen from the street. What each does follows the kind of room and what it holds there: staff stand behind the service point (the first checkout, counter or desk, on the long side with less clear floor to a wall, since the plan puts the counter so that the clerk sees the door) and work the till, a bartender wipes the bar, and a clerk and a customer at the counter talk about half the time; an office worker sits at a desk and types; customers of a dining room, a cafe or a counter service room sit at tables and booths and eat, and those of a bar sit on its stools, stand at a high table with a drink or lean on the pool table; in a shop they browse a shelf, rack, case or cooler, or wait at the till on the phone; in a laundromat they load a washer, fold at a table, wait on a bench or look at their phone; a workshop has someone at a bench. Where a room's service point is deeper than the front zone its staff work at something in it (stock a shelf, wipe a front table) or there is none. A standing person's spot is held to the floor (0.35 m in from the walls, 0.3 m from every furnishing, 0.4 m from a chair, outside the door's zone, 0.7 m from the next person) and never faces the glass unless the task is at something in the window; a person with no clear spot is not there. The hash also draws the model (a room repeats one only past five people), a standing person's facing up to 15 degrees off square, a height of 0.95 to 1.04, a clip rate of 0.85 to 1.1 and a start in the clip, so no two move in step. In the right hand a body holds a paper cup or a phone only when its clip does (`handHeld.ts`): `cup` the cup, `drink` the cup only in a cafe, a bakery, a diner, an espresso stand or a convenience store (a tavern's, a bar's or a restaurant's drink holds none), `phone`, `call` and `sit_phone` the phone, anywhere, and every other clip nothing. Each model carries the two as small primitives skinned to that hand (materials `prop.cup` and `prop.phone`, sharing one small picture); the engine would draw both on every body, so `peopleParts.ts` tells it not to draw them and draws each itself from a matrix array of its own, the size of the bodies' (the thing's instance k is body k, since a body's clip is read by its index): the body's own matrix where that body holds it, a matrix that draws nothing everywhere else.

**Where people sit is where the room is drawn with a seat.** The places chairs, stools, benches and a couch stand in are worked out in one place, `seatsOf` in `roomFill.ts` (a table's chairs, a booth's benches, a row of stools, a desk's chair, a couch, a bench, a window bar's and a high table's stools: each with its centre, the height of its top, which way a sitter faces and its family), and the drawing and `people.ts` both call it, so a sitter is on a seat that stands. A sitter's body is on the floor under the middle of the seat, raised by the seat's height less the one the clip was made for (0.46 m for a chair's clips, 0.75 m for the stool's). Seated people are not drawn on the low tier, which draws no chairs.

Each model is the engine's skinned model with its clips baked; a person is an instance with a matrix and a place in the clip of their activity, and the renderer's own clock plays them (a body that plays on costs nothing a frame). Every quarter second the people within 60 m of the eye (35 m on the low tier) are written and shown, and nothing is written in a quarter second in which the set is the same. The files are asked for once the town's geometry is made, as the vehicles' are; a model that fails to load is left out, said once. The material wears the town's air and its colour is darkened since the people stand indoors.

### People outside

The downtown's streets have people who stand or sit there for a reason (`game/town/outdoorPeople.ts`, pure, from the plan by hashes, as `people.ts` is). Those who stand or sit are slots, and those who walk are trips (below). Each slot is: a place, a reason, one of the clips the models carry (`ACTIVITIES`) and the hours they are there. `REASONS` is the table of reasons as data, a row each, to be read and changed: what the reason needs near it (an open cafe, bakery or diner in the morning; shop windows by day; the shopkeeper at their own door just after opening and before closing; a break outside an office, a bank or the post office from half past nine to half past four; a bench; a street corner's curb ramp; a tavern from early evening to closing; a restaurant or the theater in the evening; the convenience store or laundromat at night; a business that never shuts, in the dead of night; and the quay's apron at first light, by day and at dusk), where they stand against it, the clips and how often each, how many it puts at one place at once, and the bands of the day it holds with how long each stays. A slot's start, length and edges are its own by hash. A slot tied to a unit is inside that unit's hours (`unitHours`); the places are the core, dock and waterfront districts' lots, the benches and corners beside them and the quay's apron where they stand on it, and none on a house street, the highway strip or at the mill.

The clips say what a person is doing, and each reason uses the ones that say it. Waiting (lunch, a corner, errands, the theater, the quay) is `wait`, `arms`, `phone` or `call`, `idle` rarely; a break outside an office is most often a pair, else `lean`, `phone`, `call` or `cup`; a morning coffee is a pair, one of them or both with a cup; a window shopper `look`s; a shopkeeper at the door stands with `arms` or `idle`, and `wipe`s only at their own glass, an arm's length from it; a bench's sitter is `sit_rest` or `sit_phone`; the tavern's people are pairs, and so are those outside a restaurant or a cafe (below); the night shop's and the round-the-clock business's are `phone`, `call`, `lean` or `cup`, the cup only at a convenience store or a gas station; the quay's crews at first light are waiting, with arms folded, on a call or a pair with a cup each. Nobody drinks on the sidewalk: a paper cup is a morning coffee's, a break's, the quay's at first light, or at night only at a convenience store or a gas station. A pair is one who talks (`talk`) and one who listens (`listen`, or over a morning coffee sometimes `cup`), which of the two by hash; they stand 0.9 to 1.1 m apart, turned to each other and each opened 15 to 30 degrees toward the street. A leaner (`lean`) needs a wall: `leanStretches` finds, on a unit's front, the wall between its openings (a bricked-up window is wall), its door, the stair's door and a sign at a person's height, each with a quarter metre to spare and the unit's own ends, and keeps a stretch of 0.9 m or more; the leaner's back is to it, their body `LEAN_OUT` (0.345 m, which is how far the models' hips and shoulder blades stand from their origin) from its face, facing straight out a few degrees off square. Where a unit has no such stretch nobody leans there. Only a front is read: a side wall is not known as an opening or a blank, so none is leaned on.

How many is a small rural town's street, not a crowd. At one place at once: one at a shop's window (a display window; never a row), at most two on a break at one office (a pair, or 2.5 m apart), four outside a cafe, a restaurant or a tavern (two pairs), one on a bench (it is 1.76 m long), one at a corner, and two at any other unit (`unitCap`). Stays are half an hour at the least (a real minute: a game hour is two), mostly 0.6 to 1.5 hours; a bench's one to three hours, a corner's 0.3 to 0.5, so a place has fewer people in a row, each there longer. The whole downtown is held at every moment under `ceilingAt` (28 in the late morning to late afternoon, 12 at 08:00, 3 from 01:00 to 05:00, 12 again at 20:00, ramps between, scaled by the town's open downtown units against `REFERENCE_UNITS`, and two at the least): the bands' means are set under it, and a last pass drops, in the order of a hash, whoever would take it over (the two of a pair together). A small town has a floor (`DAY_FLOOR`: from half past seven to eight at night one person, from ten to five two, about six in ten of the ceiling from half past ten): where the day leaves too few out, a person waits at an open business, a corner or a bench, if one has room.

A place is clear when it is on the sidewalk or the apron, out of every roadway (the streets' own widths and the curb returns' triangles), out of every building and out of every door's way, at least 0.4 m from any prop's box (the plan's solids, the benches but their own, the sidewalk's signs, the harbour's lights, the quay's bollards), at least 1.6 m from anyone there at the same time but a pair talking, with a metre of a wide walk left to pass, and no neighbour at the same time is the same model. Everyone's height is the town ground's (the apron's deck on the quay). The player is not stopped by them.

Which way they face is set with the place. Nobody stands facing a blank wall or with their nose at a pole (`facingClear`: a metre ahead is clear of a wall, a prop's box and a post); a person who waits faces the street or along it, a person on a phone or with a cup anywhere but into the wall, a window shopper the glass of a display window from 0.8 to 1.1 m, a leaner straight out from the wall, and a bench's sitter as the bench does, with the back of the hips on the bench's back (the origin 0.14 m in front of the back's face, `BENCH_BACK` and `SIT_BEHIND`).

`peopleParts.ts` shows a person outside by their slot's hours and not by their unit's, within 110 m of the eye (60 m on the low tier; those of the rooms keep 60 and 35). So that nobody is seen to pop, a change of who is shown is taken only when the person is farther than 35 m from the eye or behind or beside the view (80 degrees either side of the way the eye looks; the field of view itself is not known to the parts); until then they stay as they were, so a smoker can outstay closing by a minute. The first time they are worked out everyone is set at once.

Outside they share the models, and so the material, of the people in the rooms (darkened by 8 percent, `INDOOR_DARKEN`, which is slight enough that a second set of models for the street was not made), and at night the town's lamps' light (`peopleLight.ts`): a body is an instance with a matrix and a place in a clip, with no colour of its own to add the lamps' sum to, so a plugin on the people's material adds it per pixel from the same list of nearest lamps (`lampLight.ts`), with the same falloff and wrap as the town's surfaces take it (the list is written by `townParts.ts` once the eye or the hour moves, and the day, with no lamp lit, skips the loop).

#### People who walk

Some of the people outside walk from one business to another (`game/town/walkers.ts`, pure and by hashes like the standers; counted beside them, not out of their ceiling). A **trip** is out of the door of one open business, along the sidewalks, across a street at a corner if it must, and in at the door of another open business; a walker is one end of a trip, or one of two who make it together (`PAIR_CHANCE`, by why they go: nine evenings out in ten, most lunches and coffees, about one errand in seven), side by side or, where a walk has no room for two abreast, one a pace behind the other on the one line. Where a walker is at an instant is a function of the day's clock alone: the trip leaves at game hour `t0` and the walker is `(hour - t0) * HOUR_S * speed` metres along a path made once, at the person's own speed, 0.95 to 1.7 m a second of real time (a trip of 150 m is about a game hour, which is right). When the console moves the hour they move with it.

`WALK_REASONS` is the table of why they go, as data: a coffee in the morning and on from the coffee to work, errands in the middle of the morning and in the afternoon between the bank, the post office, the pharmacy, the hardware store and the shops, lunch out and back, an evening at a restaurant, the tavern or the theater and on from one to another, the late convenience store, gas station or motel, and the rare one in the night. A reason says what it goes from and to and how many trips an hour the reference town has of it in each stretch of the day (a town of fewer open downtown units has fewer). Both ends are open at leaving and at arriving; a trip is 30 to 220 m of walking, the nearer ends likelier; no two trips leave one door within 20 real seconds; the whole town is held under `walkCeilingAt` (eight in the day, four or five at eight and at eight at night, one in the small hours) by dropping a trip that would go over it.

They are not a matched set. Each walker's **speed** is their own (`speedOf`: bell-shaped about 1.31 m a second, most between 1.15 and 1.5, about one in eight a slow walker under 1.1 and one in eight brisk over 1.55), and two who are out at once and come within 60 m of each other differ by at least 0.12 m a second (the later trip's speed is moved by its hashes). Each has a **line** of their own: an aim across the walk (some hug the shop fronts, some the curb, most in between), a slow wander of 0.12 to 0.25 m over 8 to 20 m about it, a way of taking a corner (a turn radius of 0.9 to 2.2 m) and a bow of up to 0.6 m across a roadway; all of it comes in over the first and last six metres, so the door's own way out and in is the usual. A faster walker who catches a slower one takes a line round them (a move of 0.9 or 1.5 m to either side) or the trip is given up. No two trips out at the same time (30 real seconds either side) share a destination or an origin, and each end has a share of the day's trips about 6 percent (`END_SHARE`); the trips are spread over every open unit that takes customers or has staff, weighting toward the ends with fewer trips. Where a town's few reachable doors cannot keep to that, a trip falls back to keeping its end a quarter of a game hour from the last trip to it (`Walker.relaxed`). A pair is two people: the gap between them breathes between 0.6 and 0.9 m, the second drifts up to 0.25 m ahead or behind over tens of seconds, and its clip rate follows its own ground speed.

The path is made through a small net (`walkNet`): a node at each curb ramp of a corner (the one the people who wait at corners stand on, or, where a corner has none, the end of the sidewalk beside it), a sidewalk edge from a corner to the next along one side of a street, a crossing edge straight across a street from the corner on one side of a leg to the corner on the other, and a short link round a corner that has no ramp. A trip is the shortest way through it from the foot of one door to the foot of the other. Along a sidewalk a walker keeps to a lane 0.35 m to the right of the middle, moved sideways by a search (`lateral`) where a pole, a bin, a bench or a person who stands is in the way, and the trip is dropped where there is no way round. Where a sidewalk was never poured they walk the strip of the right of way beside it. The polyline's corners are turned over about a metre and a half of path, and it begins 0.4 m inside the door's plane at the door's middle and ends the same way: a walker comes out through the door's leaf and goes in through it, and the doors do not open. At every instant a walker is clear of every building (0.3 m) and every prop's box (0.45 m), 0.6 m from every stander there at that hour and 0.65 m from every other walker but their partner; a trip that cannot keep that is moved in time by its hashes or dropped. The height is the ground's under them, averaged over 0.4 m of path each way, so that nobody drops 15 cm the moment they step off a curb with no ramp. A walker holds nothing.

They are drawn as bodies of the same five models playing `walk` (`peopleParts.ts`), after the standers in each model's matrices: their matrices are written every frame they are shown, and their clip floats once, when the set shown changes, at the rate that makes the file's stride (`walk` in `people.json`: the seconds of a cycle and the metres of a stride at the model's own size) agree with the walker's speed. A walker is shown from leaving the door to arriving at the other, within the same reach as the standers, and the no-pop rule does not hold them at either end. Their soft dark on the ground (`peopleShade.ts`) is written every frame with their matrices, under one key for all the walkers, at the walker's place and heading with a standing person's size and strength, and taken away the frame the last of them is not shown; the parked cars are no part of this, and what a walker does about the car that is driven is below ("How people answer the player").

#### Who is with whom

Most people are with someone. Outside a bar, a restaurant or a cafe nobody stands alone (`SOCIAL` in `outdoorPeople.ts`:
whoever is put at one, for whatever reason, is one of two who talk, and only the shopkeeper at their own door is the
exception); a break outside an office and a look at the water from the quay are most often two; and the trips that are
not errands are mostly made by two together. Alone are the ones who are shopping: at a shop's window, outside the
grocery or the hardware store, on an errand between the shops; and the shopkeepers, a bench's sitter and the one who
waits at a corner. In a bar's, a restaurant's or a cafe's room the customers come two together as well (`people.ts`): an
even number, the second of each two put where the first is (the same table, the next stool, behind them at the counter),
and where the second finds no place within 1.7 m of the first, neither is there.

#### How people answer the player

The people are a schedule: where each is at an hour is fixed, and nothing changes it. How they answer the player is the
screen's own (`game/town/peopleReact.ts`), worked out each frame from the people as they are drawn, the cars and the
local player, as what a car knocks down is. It keeps, for each person it has cause to, a small move off their schedule
and back, a turn of the body, and what they say.

- **A car.** A person in the way a moving car is going (inside the strip its body will sweep, wider when it slides, and
  less than 0.8 s ahead of its nose, at 1.2 m/s or more) jumps out of it: sideways to the nearer side that is not in a
  wall, 0.7 m clear of the strip, with a hop, in 0.36 s. They turn on the driver and say something mean (`MEAN`), stay
  out of the road while a car is near, and walk back. One in a room, behind its wall, does not; nor one who is seated.
- **The player on foot.** A person the player walks into (within 0.62 m, closing at 0.5 m/s or more) is knocked back a
  step along the player's way, leaning away, turns to them and is annoyed (`BUMPED`). Walked into again they are pushed
  further, to 1.7 m in all and never into a wall, and then go back. This is the collision there is: the person gives
  way, and the player is not stopped.
- **A greeting.** A person the player on foot comes within 3.4 m of, face to face or in passing, greets the detective by
  the hour of day (`greetingsAt`): 85 in 100 of those who walk and 40 in 100 of those who stand, the same ones each
  time; one who stands turns to them. Not the same person twice within a minute and a half, nor two people at once.
- **Talk.** Two who stand, sit or walk together, with the player within 8 m, say a line now and then (4.5 to 9 s apart),
  in turn, of the kind of place they are at (`CHATTER`: a bar, a cafe, a restaurant, work, a shop or the street).

What is said is not voiced: a line is a caption over the head of the one who says it (`game/town/speech.ts` places it
on the frame's view, `hud.ts` shows it), no more than four at once, fading with distance to nothing at 26 m. The words
are `game/peopleWords.ts`, tables to be read and changed, a line chosen by a hash of who says it and how many they have
said. No clip is made for a jump or a stumble (the models carry none): the body is shifted, hopped, leaned and turned by
its matrix as it plays the clip it was playing (`PeopleParts.setNudge`, `writeNudged`), and what it holds goes with it.
`globalThis.__capePeople` answers a measuring tool with who is drawn, what is being said and how far a person is moved,
and can provoke a reaction by the frame's own rules.

### Buildings from the plan

`plan.uses` (units, rooms, signs, window records) is drawn by `game/town/`, in this order for each building: the walls are cut around their openings (`buildingKit.ts` `cutWall`: a window is a hole with a reveal), each opening is filled, then the roof, the parts and the signs.

- **Fronts** (`frontGeometry.ts`). A commercial building's street wall is its units: each opening where the plan puts it (a widened one in the frame of its remodel's decade, a bricked one filled in newer brick with its lintel showing, a papered one with the paper and a corner down, a boarded one in plywood), its door where the plan puts it (recessed with returns and a step when the plan says), a bulkhead (a panel on a front of before 1960, a granite base after), pilasters between units of an old brick front, a corbelled cornice in one of four shapes by the building, the stair door, awnings (canvas in a few colours, a third faded, some torn, some only the ribs) and canopies, and the upper storeys' windows from the plan's records (some of them the vinyl of an owner's remodel).
- **Rooms** (`roomGeometry.ts`, `roomShell.ts`, `roomLight.ts`, `roomDressing.ts`, `roomKit.ts`). Behind each unit's glass the plan's room is built and lit by its own lights, baked into vertex colour. `roomLight.ts` makes a field over the room's plan (a lattice of colours, 0.75 m on the full tier): the plan's lights that are on (a tube's cool white, a pendant's and lamp's warm, neon's own colour, with a falloff and an exposure that brings the sum of a full set to one mean), a failed tube (one in about ten in a room with many tenants behind it, from the unit's record) leaving a dim patch, the day coming in through each opening (less through paper, blinds or a display) and fading with depth, a small ambient, a lamp spilling from the back room; and the furnishings' shadows on the floor. `roomShell.ts` builds the floor, ceiling and the three walls as grids of quads (about 1 m on the full tier, 2 to 5 m on the low) whose corners take the field's light (`MeshBuilder.litGrid`, glow 1: the baked light is the whole light), on the town's scanned layers where one fits (plank floors, concrete, painted plaster, panelling) tinted by the unit's own record (a vacant unit keeps its last tenant's paint, faded and stained; a seventies remodel has panelling; an old building has plank floors; the trade picks the colours), a baseboard, a wainscot or a stripe where the trade has one, and a doorway at the back (open on a lit back room or a shut door). `roomDressing.ts` hangs what each trade has on its walls (a menu board and clock in a cafe, a pegboard in a hardware store, bottles against a mirror and a lit beer sign in a tavern, a calendar and a licence in an office, a price list over the washers, rods in a tackle shop), the window's plant, the display window's goods on a low backdrop by trade, and a vacant unit's ghosts (the pale patches and brackets of shelving that came down). The furnishings (`roomGeometry.ts`) are lit by the same field at each corner: shelf runs with packages on their long faces, counters with a till, tables with chairs, booths, stools, display cases, coolers with lit fronts, washers with round doors, desks, racks, crates, a ladder, paint cans, a lift with a car. Only the faces a person outside can see are built. The low tier keeps its rooms coarse (a grid of a few quads a face, no doorway, no dressing). What stands round and on the furnishings is denser in the band too (a coat over a chair's back, a newspaper and cups on a table, clutter at a counter's street end, boxes by a tall shelf, a broom, a bucket, a hand-written sign on a stand, hours or a flyer taped inside the glass at the door's end). The back is not drawn at all: a room is seen only through its glass, so only its front is there. The lights are on to the back wall and the room is bright, but from 1.1 m behind the glass everything runs to a veil, the room's own light as one even tone, and by 2.2 m it is whole (`roomLight.ts`: `veil`, `VEIL_FROM`, `VEIL_TO`, the field's `veil` colour, laid on every lit colour by `roomKit.ts`'s `shade`). At 2.4 m (`BACKDROP_AT`) the room as drawn ends at a flat sheet (`roomBackdrop.ts`) that carries the rest of the room as a picture out of focus: a coarse lattice of colours, 0.3 m apart, painted in one-point perspective with the floor, the side walls, the ceiling and the back wall, each furnishing that stands further in as a soft block of its own colour (a shelf run's is speckled with its trade's goods) and each working lamp as a glow, laid mostly into the veil and wholly into it at its edges, on a material whose grain is magnified to a soft mottle. Between the glass and the sheet the room's air is drawn too (`roomHaze.ts`): nine see-through sheets across the room (four on the low tier), each the veil's colour and each taking a little more of what is behind it than the one before, thin at the glass and thick at the back, 58 percent in all by the sheet. They are what makes the front, the veil and the picture one thing: a floor's grain, a chair's edges and the people go by degrees with depth, and the painted sheet is met with no line. The sheets are a layer of their own (`haze`), blended and drawn before the lettering and the glass (`townParts.ts`, `HAZE_ORDER`), the furthest first; they use the glass's material, told from a pane by a seed of 2, with their colour in the floats a pane's size and tilt use (`townMaterial.ts`). Nothing has an edge on the sheet, which is what a steamed pane does to a lit room; it is there so that a room need be furnished only at its front and still read as lit and full. A furnishing wholly behind the sheet, anything on the back wall, the back wall and its doorway are not drawn; a box that stands through the sheet is drawn to it; a small furnishing takes the veil at one depth, so that two of its faces that lie over each other are one colour. **Where the renderer keeps the frame the opaque draws left, none of that is drawn and the room is read through its glass out of focus** (`townBlur.ts`; the engine's `RenderMakeLate.behind`, which the high tier has for its water and the medium and low tiers keep because the town's side says `keepsFrame`: the low tier, which has no post chain, breaks its scene's pass itself and its own line pass reads the kept depth): the room is drawn whole to its back wall with its lights on, with no veil, sheet or haze (`townGeometry`'s `through`, down to `RoomIn.through`), and each pane with a room behind it is marked (`PaneLook.through`, a seed of 4 to 5). A marked pane is an opaque draw in a layer of its own (`through`, beside the blended `glass` that the other panes keep), drawn first in the half of the frame after the break (`afterBreak`): it writes its own depth, the plane of the glass, and does not blend over the room: it reads the room from the kept frame, 28 reads of the colour over a disc whose radius is 0.45 m at the place read, by how far behind the pane that place is by the kept depth (sharp at the glass, soft from 0.25 m, as soft as it gets by 1.3 m). A read counts by how soft the place it lands on is itself, so a sharp thing at the window does not smear into the room, and a read in front of the pane's own plane (the frame, the wall) does not count. The disc is turned by another angle at each pixel and each frame, which the renderer's blending of frames in time smooths. What is read is brighter the deeper it is and takes a little haze, both by the room's own brightness; the pane then lays its film, dirt and reflection over it and writes the whole pixel, so the lettering is drawn after the glass on this tier (and stands in front of the pane's plane, which the depth now holds: the OPEN sign hung in a window keeps its box 2 cm behind the pane and has its lit lettering on the pane's face, since anything behind the plane is seen only through the pane, `shopSigns.ts`). Every pixel of a marked pane is composed from the kept frame (the glass's own code makes every one of them glass; a pixel that was not would be the kept frame's own, sharp, and hide nothing). The pane also writes, into the frame's alpha, how much of the lines behind it it hides: how out of focus it reads the place, 0 at the glass and 1 where the blur is whole. **That is what tells the ink line** (the engine's `finish.lineThrough`, set in `look.ts`): the line is drawn from the depth, and the depth at a marked pane is the pane's own, so the far softening also reads the depth the first half left (the room's) and the pass's own, to find where a surface covers what the first half drew, and draws the room's edges there by what the nearest of them leaves of them: a thing at the glass keeps its full line, a thing out of focus has less by degrees, and one whose picture is whole has none, while the window's frame, the wall's edges and a person in front of the glass are not behind the pane and are drawn as before. The tier with no kept frame draws the line from the depth alone, as before. All three tiers read the room this way. The opening of a shop's door whose leaf swings is read the same way with no glass over it: it has a marked pane of its own that is nothing but the read (see "Doors": `OPEN_SEED`). The low tier differs in two things: it reads the frame 16 times a pixel, not 28 (`TAPS_LOW`), over a smaller disc (`BLUR_M_LOW`, `BLUR_MAX_PX_LOW`: its reads are wider apart), and it lifts what it reads (`ROOM_GAMMA_LOW`, `ROOM_GAIN_LOW`: that tier's kept frame is the display's colour, tone mapped, not the chain's linear one, so a lit room read from it came out darker than the other tiers' at noon and far darker at night), and it turns the disc by the pixel alone, since it has no blending of frames in time and a disc that turned every frame would shimmer; its rooms are the low tier's coarse ones (the largest few furnishings, a grid of a few quads a face, no dressing), drawn whole to the back wall. The veil, the haze sheets and the painted sheet are what a renderer with no kept frame draws (no texture arrays, `through: false`): they stay in the code, and no tier of the game draws them now. A vacant unit has no veil and no sheet: its bareness is the point, and there a furnishing whose centre is deeper than `PLAIN_FROM` (6.5 m, `roomFill.ts`) is still its big shape only; `seatsOf` gives no seat to a plain furnishing, so what is drawn and what people sit on stay one thing.
- **Hours** (`hours.ts`, `townMaterial.ts`, `people.ts`). Each unit with a tenant has opening hours from its trade's line in a table authored there (a tavern 11 to 2, a bank 9 to 17, a bakery 6 to 15; a gas station, motel, hotel, rooming house, lodge, bail bonds and self storage are always open, and so is any unit of a building that is never shut for the day), its opening and closing each moved by an hour either way by a hash of the unit's id, so that a street does not go dark in one minute. A unit the plan has shut for the day or vacant has none and stays unlit at every hour. The hours ride into the shader as one number in float 10 of every layered vertex of the unit's room (`MeshBuilder.withHours`, laid by `roomGeometry`; 0 is none, 600 always, otherwise `1 + open * 24 + close`, exact in a 16-bit float). The engine's hour of day (the console's `time`) reaches the game's render side with the view each frame (`side.wildlife.look`), and `townParts.ts` writes it to the town's materials as `townHour` when it has moved by a hundredth. Where a layered vertex has a code, the shader keeps its glow (a room's baked light) by how open the unit is, and hands the rest of the colour back to the scene's light at a share (`SHUT_LIGHT`, a little blue): a shut room is lit by the day or the night outside and not by itself, so it is dim at noon and as dark as the street's shadows at midnight (a share of its own glow would stand as bright at midnight as at noon, and brighter to the eye, since the frame's exposure follows the sky into the dark). The lights come up and go down over a sixth of an hour either side of the hour. A pane's reflected sky and its film, authored at a noon's brightness, follow the sky's own at the hour (the air's far colour over `NOON_SKY`), so a dark room's window is dark and a lit one's shows. `people.ts` gives each person their unit's hours, and `peopleParts.ts` shows only those whose unit is open at the hour (`isOpen`), worked out again on its quarter-second step and at once when the hour moves. The low tier's haze sheets keep their colour in the floats the code would use, so on that tier a shut room's haze stays lit.
- **The sky a wall sees** (`skyShade.ts`, `footShade.ts`, `meshBuilder.ts`, `townMaterial.ts`). The town has no shadows and no pass that darkens a corner, so the builders work out where a wall's corner stands and the corner carries it. Each corner of a wall has two numbers in the two floats a textured corner had to spare: its height over the ground (float 10, kept under a half, a metre of it 1/256: the float is also a room's hours, which are 1 or more, and the material tells them apart by that) and its depth under its roof (float 11, which the flat ground uses for its depth rank and does not read as a code). The material turns the two into the share of the sky's light the pixel takes: 45 percent at the ground rising to all of it 0.8 m up, and the same under the roof over 0.9 m. It scales the sky's fill light (every light but the sun, at `CUSTOM_FRAGMENT_LIGHT`), its ambient light and its reflection by that share, and leaves the sun's light and the surface's own colour alone. A wall under two roof planes (a gable end) is cut along the line where they meet, so each piece's depth is one plane's. A pitched roof has an underside, which sees none of the sky, and a board along each eave and up each gable. On the ground the same contact is a see-through sheet (`footShade.ts`): a strip along every wall that has open ground before it, one dark colour covering most at the wall and nothing 0.75 m out, with a fan at each outer corner, its reach and depth the building's own by a roll on its id. The ground under a wall may be a sidewalk, a yard or the engine's terrain, so the strip is laid over it and not cut into it: the sheets are a layer of their own (`shade`, in `townGeometry.ts`, marked by a seed of 3 in the float a pane keeps its seed in), drawn blended before the haze and the glass and a little ahead of the flat ground in depth (`townParts.ts`). The low tier has no strips. **What else stands on the ground has the same dark under it.** A parked vehicle (`props/vehicleShade.ts`) has one sheet of shared corners in the same `shade` layer, a rounded rectangle of its footprint in six rings: full under the body and falling smoothly (v from 1 to 0, so the sheet's alpha is `dark * v^2` as the strips') to nothing 0.35 to 0.7 m past the sills and bumpers, so it has no edge and no seam. Its reach and depth are its kind's and its lift's, moved by a roll on its id (a sedan 0.42 m and 0.94; a pickup 0.54 and 0.82; a work truck 0.58 and 0.78; a car on blocks wider and lighter, a derelict on flat tyres narrower and darker). The ground's height is asked at each corner (the road's crown and a slope are followed); a corner more than 35 cm off the plane under the vehicle (the end of a deck, a ditch) is held on the plane and is clear, so it never hangs over a drop; at a curb the sheet climbs the curb's face in the one ring that crosses it. It is written for what the plan parks, model or stand-in alike, with the town's geometry (about 120 vertices a vehicle, 0.5 percent of the town's vertices); a vehicle that moves would need the kind a person has. A boat trailer (`plan.props.trailers`, with or without a boat) has the same sheet (`trailerShade`) on its own footprint, with a pickup's numbers (reach 0.54 m, depth 0.8) because its bed stands high and open, and lighter for a bare one (0.5 m, 0.64). The ragged stain a vehicle always had stays under it, in the flat layer. A person (`peopleShade.ts`) is not town geometry, since people are shown and hidden by the hour and by distance and will come to walk: one draw of thin instances of one quad, a place a person shown (`ShadePlace`: where, which way they face, two half sizes, a strength) written by `peopleParts.ts` whenever it writes the set of a model (`PeopleShade.write`, a key for each writer), so a body that moves later writes its own place each frame. Its material is the walls' feet's in effect (the same colour, blended, never written to depth, drawn at `SHADE_ORDER + 0.5` among the see-through draws and `SHADE_AHEAD_UNITS` ahead of the ground in depth), with the falloff worked out in the pixel from where it is on the quad and the strength carried in the instance's data. A standing person's is 0.9 by 0.7 m across its rim and 0.62 deep at the middle (mostly under the feet and the legs), a sitter's larger. People indoors have it too: the glass is drawn after it. Like the walls' feet it is one colour at any hour (the air's fog over it makes it the night's), and the low tier has none of the three.
- **Accent colours** (`accents.ts`, used by `frontGeometry.ts`, `openingsGeometry.ts`, `shopSigns.ts`, `props/vehicleLook.ts`). The town spends colour on about a tenth of the picture and keeps the rest quiet. There are three families, each a faded paint and never a fresh primary: a faded red, a mustard and a harbour blue. Of the things that can carry one (a door, a sign's ground, an awning, the base of a shopfront, a vehicle) 28 percent do, rolled from the thing's own id; the family is its block's own three times in four, so that one family leads a block; and each use is faded a further 10 to 35 percent toward its own grey and never shown at more than 0.6 saturation. The saturated colours that were there before (canvas, sign grounds) are taken 45 percent toward grey, so that the accents are what holds colour.
- **Relief** (`windowGeometry.ts`, `frontGeometry.ts`, `buildingParts.ts`, `buildingKit.ts`). The look's ink line (`look.ts`) is found from depth: a step is drawn when it is deeper than about a hundredth of the distance it is seen from. So the parts a real building has deep are deep here. A window's opening is 24 cm deep in a wall of brick, stone, stucco or concrete and 16 cm in one of boards or sheet metal (`revealOf`), with the glass 9 cm short of its end (`glassOffOf`), and everything hung in a window or stood in a shop's window is set from the glass, so it moves with it. A sill stands 16 cm out, the lintel over an upper brick window is a block 12 cm deep, a pilaster 16 cm, the water table 12 cm, and a cornice's bands 14 to 26 cm under a cap of 34 cm (38 cm under a pitched roof, to clear the roof's edge board). A house's corner boards are 6 cm, the one at a wall's far end wrapping the corner so that two boards never share a plane, and a door's casing 6 and 7 cm. The low tier keeps its shallower windows and trim.
- **Windows and glass** (`windowGeometry.ts`, `windowDressing.ts`, `townMaterial.ts`). Glass is a quad in a separate blended layer drawn after the opaque ones (a shop's panes, where the renderer reads the frame behind its glass, are an opaque layer of their own instead: see Rooms). It reflects by direction: the eye's ray is turned off the pane's normal (the wall's, tilted by a small fixed turn each pane has, more on old glass), and above the horizon the pane shows the overcast sky (paler toward the horizon), at or below it the dark, uneven band of the street's far side; how much it reflects is a floor (more for the sky) plus Schlick's fresnel, so a shop window seen from the sidewalk shows its lit room, an upper window the sky with the room faint behind it, and glass along its length is a mirror. Grime (a band at the foot, the lower corners, streaks) is laid over it from the pane's `dirt` and own `seed` (per vertex: the pane's uv, size and tilt ride in the floats a flat vertex does not use). House and upper windows are holes with trim, a sill, glass and a shallow box behind it lit by a gradient (a lamp now and then), with the plan's treatment: curtains in folds, blinds of separate slats part way down (one end sagging, a slat bent), vertical blinds, a lit room, a dark one, boards, or broken glass; a plant or a lamp on the sill, and now and then a sheet of cardboard in a pane, a window air-conditioner or a box fan. A vacant unit's paper is sheets with seams, tape, a torn corner, faded toward the top. Every layer of a front lies on a step of depth 2.5 to 3 cm from the next (the ladder: 3, 6, 9, 12 cm out from a wall; behind the glass 2.5 cm a layer), because the depth buffer (24 bits, near 0.05 m) holds two faces apart only when they are 4 steps apart at 60 m (2 cm); `test/townWorld/depthSeparation.test.ts` fails when two faces that look the same way overlap, 0.25 m^2 or more, closer than 2 cm. House and upper windows on side and back walls are the low tier's (no trim). A wall that stands within 0.7 m of another building has no opening.
- **Signs and lettering** (`shopSigns.ts`, `signStyle.ts`, `signAtlas.ts`, `signPaint.ts`). Each `Sign` is a backing of solid geometry (board, box, post, bracket) and a face: a quad in the sign layer mapped to a rectangle of a lettering atlas (4096 wide; 2048 on low), a plan of shelf-packed rectangles each with a `SignSpec` (the plan's lines, style, decade, condition, lit, colours, a seed). The atlas is only as tall as what it holds: signs are placed while the town is built in a space as tall as the tier's atlas (2048; 1024 on low), and when every sign is placed `SignAtlas.fit` packs the same rectangles again, tallest first, into the fewest rows (a multiple of 64 texels, never more than the tier's own height; `place` never refuses, taking shelves below the space when it is full, and only if the packed rows would pass the height are the smallest faces dropped, last placed first among equals, and their quads taken out of the batches), (leaving room around a face that is drawn past its rectangle, a banner's sag, found by `reach`) and `carry` moves the UV of every sign quad (their sign layer's batches are held until then) to the packed place. The texture, its paint surface and the pixels uploaded are made at that height, after the town's geometry and before the sign batches become meshes, so no sign is drawn before its lettering; the surface and its pixels are let go when the upload is done. The renderer paints the atlas with a 2D canvas (`signPaint.ts`) in slices of the build and uploads it once; with no canvas the requests are the record. The face, weight, case, layout and colours are rolled from the sign's own seed and its style and decade (`signStyle.ts`: generic font stacks with fallbacks, never a file). Kinds: fascia, banner (sagging, a corner loose), blade on a bracket, lettering on the glass (gold leaf on an arc on an old front), paint on a wall, ghost signs, poles (the gas station's price rows), sandwich boards, OPEN, FOR LEASE, civic boards. Condition fades, peels, runs rust and dirt, kills neon letters and drops those of a box, yellows plastic and cracks or removes a panel. A lit box glows evenly, lit neon only in its tubes. Small faces (a notice, the hours plate on a door, a sticker: up to 64 texels tall) have a strip of their own at the foot of the planning space, so that they do not break the big signs' shelves; the packing then puts them with the rest. Beyond the plan's signs each unit's glass carries the hours on the door (OPEN, or CLOSED and when it is back), flyers and community notices taped crooked at the edges of the glass or onto the boards of a boarded front, and stickers by the door, painted at 2 to 2.5 times the atlas's density (`windowLife`); faces that would fall on one another each stand a step in front of the last.
- **Wear by cause** (`buildingWear.ts`). The foot of a wall is a band of its own (dirt splash and algae on the shaded side), above it a wooden wall peels on the south-west faces, masonry greens on the north ones and metal rusts in streaks; dirt runs from under the eave and in a streak under each sill (a decal in the wall's material); roofs are mossy on the north slope and rust in streaks where metal. A per-building roll scales the dirt (an owner who washed the front).
- **Parts** (`buildingParts.ts`). Houses: foundation, corner boards, steps or a porch (one in six closed in), a chimney, gutters and downspouts, a meter and conduit, a heat pump or propane tank, a lean-to or carport of another siding and roof, a patch of newer siding, a patch of new roof or a tarp weighed with tires. Flat roofs: a hatch, vents, air handlers, a fire escape on old brick blocks. By kind: a gas station's canopy and pumps, a motel's covered walkway and doors, a church's bell cote (a landmark church has a steeple instead: a tower of the church's own walls entered by a pair of doors at its foot, with louvres at the bells and a shingled spire, `buildingGeometry.ts`), a fire hall's hose tower, a portico on a bank, courthouse, lodge hall, school or hotel, a warehouse's ridge vent.
- **Tiers.** The low tier draws a unit's furnishings as the six largest simple forms (no goods, chairs or garments), windows without reveal-trim or sills or a room box, no corner boards, meters, gutters, repairs or sill streaks, and a smaller atlas (36 texels a metre). Authored, not researched: every size, colour, glow, chance and roll in these files; each says in a comment which named thing a roll stands for.

### The day, the dark and the lamps

- **The day's clock** (`client/src/game/dayClock.ts`, wired in `game.ts`). The hour of day moves on its own: the console's hour (`SessionHost.base.hour`, set by `/time`) plus the world's ticks since it was set, at `HOUR_S = 120` real seconds to a game hour (a 48 minute day), wrapped to 0 up to 24. It counts ticks (`state.tick`, which every peer shares, with the engine's `TICK_DT`), never wall-clock time, so every player sees one hour; a new console hour restarts the clock from it at the tick it is first seen. The game's half of a session (`walkSide`, made from the host) does it in `beforeSync`: only when the hour is more than a hundredth of an hour (round the circle) from `host.appliedHour` does it set that and call `renderer.setView`, which recomputes the sky.
- **Night codes** (`hours.ts`). A second band of codes above the shops' (`ALWAYS_CODE` 600), 601 to 2000, for what follows the dark: an on-step (50 of them, four minutes apart, from half an hour before dusk) and an off-step (the quarter hours from 20:00 to 02:30, and the last, dawn). `nightCode` makes one, `nightShare(code, hour)` reads it (0 by day, 1 between its times, a two minute fade each side; a pair whose off is before its on is never lit), and the shader's decode (`HOURS_CODE` in `townMaterial.ts`) is the same arithmetic from the same constants. Dusk and dawn (`DUSK_HOUR`, `DAWN_HOUR`) are where the engine's own sun (`sunAltitudeDeg`) crosses -2 degrees, and a test fails if the engine's sun moves them. The code rides in float 10 of a layered vertex, as a shop's hours do (a flat vertex's float 10 is a wire's radius or a pane's tilt); `MeshBuilder.withHours` lays it and `withGlow` gives a polygon a glow where the builder (a box, a cylinder) cannot say it. A surface with a night code and a glow under 1 is lit from within only at night (a window's cloth); one with a glow of 1 or more is a lamp's lens, dull grey glass by day.
- **Street lamps** (`sim/town/port/props/poles.ts`, `game/town/props/poleGeometry.ts`, `props/lamps.ts`). The plan puts a light on a pole at every junction of the highway, an avenue or a main street (a pole of its own where none is near), at about half of the other street junctions, and along an avenue or main street wherever the next light is over about 70 m off. Which lamp is dead (one in twelve), white (one in five of the rest) or sodium orange is the drawing's, from the pole's seed (`lampKind`), and each lamp comes on at its own step like a photocell and stays on until dawn. The highway's own poles are the world's power line (`powerLine.ts`) and carry no lights, so a stretch of highway between junctions is dark. A harbour light and a pier light shine too; a day beacon does not.
- **The light they throw** (`lampLight.ts`, `townMaterial.ts`, `townParts.ts`). `lampSites` lists every lamp (position of the lens, colour, range, strength, night code): the street lamps, the harbour's lights and each home's porch light (range 5 m). Once a frame, when the eye or the hour has moved, `fillLamps` takes the nearest `MAX_LAMPS` (24; 12 on the low tier) lit lamps to the eye, each with its strength times its `nightShare`, and fading to nothing as its distance nears the cut-off (the lesser of 130 m and the distance of the first lamp left out), so no lamp pops in or out. The list is written as uniforms (`lampP<i>`, `lampC<i>`: a vec4 each, since the material's buffer takes no array) with `townLamps` the count, and the fragment code adds to a layered surface's glow its own colour times each lamp's colour and strength, a falloff (full to a quarter of the range, then smooth to nothing) and a soft wrap of how much it faces the lamp. With no lamp lit (the day) the loop is skipped. It lights the street, the sidewalk, the pole and the walls near it; it does not light people, vehicles, trees or the engine's own ground outside the town, and it is not shadowed (a wall is lit by a lamp on its far side only through the wrap). The exposure follows the sky into the dark, so the strengths (`LAMP_GAIN`, `LENS_GLOW`, the windows' glow) were set by looking at the town at night.
- **Homes** (`windowNight.ts`). By day no home window glows from within. In the evening each occupied home lights some of its curtained and blinded windows, each with its own night code (on from half an hour before dusk to two hours after; off about the household's bedtime, 21:00 to 01:00; a few until dawn); the household decides how many (an old couple one, a family most) and the room they live in faces the street. A window that is not lit has a code that is never lit, so no covering glows at night by itself. A vacant, boarded or ruined house lights none, and nor do an office's upper windows. A home without a porch of its own has a porch light by its door (a lens with a night code, and a lamp of the list).
- **Shop signs** (`shopSigns.ts`). A lit sign follows its unit's hours: an OPEN sign only while the unit is open; another lit sign while it is open and, for about half the shops, one to three hours after it shuts if that is in the evening, never in the small hours; a trade that never shuts keeps its sign on; a unit that is shut for the day has none. The sign layer's vertices are flat, so its material (`townSurfaceCode(low, true)`) decodes the same hours on them, and not the blinds' stripes.

### Faces the depth buffer cannot tell apart

The depth buffer is 24 bits with the near plane at 0.05 m, so a step of depth is z * z / (0.05 * 2^24) metres at a distance z (1.1 mm at 30 m, 4.3 mm at 60 m, 12 mm at 100 m, 27 mm at 150 m). Two faces that look the same way and overlap flicker as the camera moves unless they are held apart: **2 cm** (four steps at 60 m) for what is within reach of the street, **2.5 cm** above 4 m over the ground or the sea (more than one step to 145 m). Layers on a wall stand on a ladder from the wall's face: 3, 6, 9 and 12 cm (2.5 cm a layer behind glass); a sign's lettering is 3.5 cm in front of its board; a board's front is 3 cm behind the face on it and clear of any other face on the wall (`stepOff` in `shopSigns.ts`, which every sign's face, board front and banner goes through). Where two volumes meet (a box against a wall) one is sunk into the other, or the hidden face is not drawn (a quay's face stops under its cap; a run of fence along a building's wall is not drawn; a patch of paved ground is cut out under the buildings, so a room's floor is not drawn over by ground the flat layer pulls forward). What is dropped at random (a ruin's rubble, the hatch, vents and air handlers of a flat roof) is tried again clear of what is there, and given up after a few draws. `MeshBuilder.finish` leaves out a triangle that repeats one already laid with the same look, and the corners nothing uses.

`test/townWorld/depthSeparation.test.ts` holds the whole town to this (`nearPlanes.ts` finds the pairs: faces grouped over a 3 m grid, their planes compared where they overlap along the normal; `townFaces.ts` builds the town and joins pairs into patches of 25 cm^2 or more). The town's materials cull back faces (none is `doubleSided`), so faces that look opposite ways are no pair. Two flat faces of one colour are not counted (they cannot be told apart). Nothing is left: every count is 0 on five seeds on the full tier and one on the low. **The flat ground is ordered by rank, not by millimetres.** The flat layer is drawn a fixed number of depth steps ahead of the terrain (`ahead`, `townParts.ts`), which is nothing between two flat faces. Each flat vertex carries a depth rank (`RANK` in `groundSurfaces.ts`; float 11 of the vertex's plugin data), and the flat material's vertex stage draws a vertex of rank r `RANK_UNITS` * r (6 * r) steps further ahead (`townMaterial.ts`): steps of depth, so the order holds at every distance (a step is z * z / (0.05 * 2^24) m at a distance z), with no mesh and no draw added. Base surfaces are rank 0; marks, cuts, seams, cracks and paint are 1 to 5; the dark under a vehicle 7; shoulders tucked under the asphalt -1. `Surface.lay` cuts a mark off the marks of its rank (the first laid keeps its ground), so no two overlap at one rank. That step is the same number of depth units at every distance, so a flat face is pulled forward by about 1.4e-4 * z^2 metres (1.4 cm at 10 m, 13 cm at 30 m, 1.4 m at 100 m): a solid face that lies within 2.45 cm of the flat ground is held apart by the finder, and one that lies 2.5 to 5 cm over it is covered by the flat ground beyond about 100 to 200 m. The wall faces of a building (the trim, plates, frames, stickers and signs) are held 2.45 cm apart by a ledger (`faceLedger.ts`) that moves a face laid over another's place a step (3 cm) out. **Paint on a standing face is ordered by rank too, and lies flush.** A wall's tone (the dark lower third, patches, a ghost sign's rows), the streak under a sill, a patch of siding, a painted sign, the marks on a foundation and the moss on a roof have no thickness, so they are not laid off their face on the ladder: each lies 2 mm a rank off the face it is painted on (`paint.ts`, `PAINT_STEP`), with no step of depth for the line the ink effect draws to outline, which would read as a board fixed to the wall. A painted face carries its rank of paint in the alpha of its vertices' colour (`Fx.paint`; 1 for everything that is not paint, `1 - rank / PAINT_RANKS` for rank 1 to 15, 16 ranks; the town opts in to no vertex alpha, so nothing else reads it), and the vertex stage of every town material that is not the flat ground's draws a vertex `PAINT_UNITS` (2) steps per rank further ahead (`PAINT_CODE`, `townMaterial.ts`: the same form and sign as `RANK_CODE`, with `K = PAINT_UNITS * PAINT_RANKS / 2^24` on `1 - color.a`), which is nothing for rank 0. Two steps are 2.4 cm at 100 m, under the 6 cm that trim stands proud of its wall, so a rank-1 sheet is never drawn through trim. The ranks run outward from the face: the wall's tone 1, a sill's streak 2, a patch of siding 3, a painted or ghost sign 4 (its lettering is in the one face of the atlas, and a sign painted over another is one rank above it); on a foundation the pours and a skirt's dark 1, the lines (courses, cracks, the vent, a skirt's boards) 2, algae 3 and mud 4; on a roof the first patch of moss 1 and the second 2. A sheet of paint is no face of the ledger: it neither notes itself nor is pushed out by one, and it is left out where a face of the ledger already stands over its place (`PROBE` in `wallTone.ts`) as a real thing is. A painted sign over a board on the wall (the plywood over a window) lies on that board, 2 mm a rank off the board's own face (`faceUnder`, `faceLedger.ts`). What stands off a wall as a thing stays as it was (a sheet of plywood nailed over a repair, plywood over a window, boards, trim, sign boxes, the crawl-space hatch). The finder reads a face's rank of paint from its alpha, and two faces of the standing layers whose ranks of paint differ are held apart by the rank and are not a pair; two of one rank that overlap are, so no two sheets of one rank overlap (a sheet looks for the room it takes before it is laid).

### The railroad and the power line

The railroad's main line runs the whole coast, beside the highway on its seaward side (`rail.ts`: the highway's centre less `RAIL_OFFSET`, 16 m), and the power line runs beside the highway on its inland side. Neither begins or ends at the town: the town's own tracks leave the main line, and its streets cross it.

- **The bed** (`railGround.ts`, composed into the place's `shapeD` after the town's ground). The engine lays the highway's corridor over a place's ground within 30 m of its centre, which takes in the line, so the bed answers `staged + k (g - staged)`, `g` the grade line and `k` the bed's weight: the corridor's own weight then cancels and the ground the engine draws is `(1 - k) N + k g`. The bench is exactly the highway's grade line for that z (`RAIL_BENCH_HALF` 2.75 m either side, flat across, the grade line's slope along), falling or rising to the land over a bank whose width grows with how far the grade line stands from the land (2 m on level ground, up to 30 m, cubic ease), so a cut or a fill is never needlessly steep. The gradient is exact (tested against finite differences) and a sample farther than `RAIL_REACH` (33 m) of the line returns the town's answer as the same object after one comparison. Measured on ten seeds along 6 km: the bench differs from the grade line by under 4e-15 m; the shaping stage costs 0.07 us far from the line (as before), and 0.16 us on and beside it against 0.05 to 0.07 before, of a whole terrain sample of 1.2 us.
- **The sea** (`railSpans.ts`). ## The car

One car can be driven: a sedan that stands at the curb of the street nearest the municipal building's door. `carStart(seed)`
(`client/src/sim/car/start.ts`) finds it, a pure function of the world's seed like the town's start: the nearest street
of kind main, street or avenue to the plan's spawn, then the first place along it, at 0, +6.5, -6.5, +13 m and so on to
78 m either way, where the car's three circles touch no building and no prop and its footprint stays a hull's radius from the spawn. The car lies 1.2 m inside the curb on the
spawn's side with the curb on its right; if no place is free it stands at the spawn's foot on the street's centre line.
The world holds the cars in `state.cars` (`createWalkWorld` makes one, with id 1), and the wire, the rules and the
drawing all take a list, though the game has one.

The code is `client/src/sim/car/` (the simulation, which every machine computes alike and which the rules of
`AGENTS.md`'s "The simulation's rules" hold), `client/src/game/car/` (what is drawn, heard and shown), and the car's
lines in `sim.ts`, `net.ts`, `game.ts`, `hud.ts` and `townParts.ts`.

### The engine's hook, and why the car runs everywhere

The car rides on the engine's `Rules.move` (`sim/tick.ts`): a game's own move for a player, which the engine calls first
for every player and every command, and which, when it answers true, stands in for the walking step, the `constrain` and
the lakes' walls. The engine's tick also lets a game say a player is `held` (it takes its command's look and moves no
further) and gives the host a `hostStep` after every player has moved. The car needs an engine that has `Rules.move`.

`carMove` (`sim/car/rules.ts`) is that move. For a player who is seated it drives the car from the command if the player
holds seat 0, and puts every seated player at their seat; for anyone else it answers false and the player walks. Because
the engine runs a player's move on the host, on a client's own predicted copy of its player and in a client's replay of
the commands the host has not yet acknowledged, the car's step runs there too. A joined player's driving is therefore
predicted at once and replayed on a correction, never waited for from the host; this is why `driveCar` is a pure
function of the world's seed and the car, and why nothing under `sim/car/` reads a clock, a random number or a
collection's order. `carHostStep` and `carPressed` run on the host alone.

A host tick that has no fresh command from a joined driver passes the engine's standing command (one with the sequence
number of the last one taken), and the car steps on it as a walker does, on the driver's last controls. The car records that it did: `owed`, a count from 0 to `OWED_MAX` (6, a tenth of a
second) of steps taken in advance. The command that comes late is fresh, and while the car is owed a step it only sets the
controls and pays one back, without stepping again. So a driver's N commands step the car N times however the ticks fell,
and the host's car and the client's prediction come to the same place. A client's replay of its unacknowledged commands
swallows the same steps, because `owed` is part of the car it is corrected to. Once the car is owed `OWED_MAX` steps a
silent driver's car is braked (the handbrake, no throttle) instead of repeating the last controls, so a driver whose tab has gone to
the background leaves a car that comes to rest and stays there; when commands come again, at most `OWED_MAX` are swallowed
and then the car answers. A new driver, or none, owes nothing.

### The state and the ride

A `Car` (`sim/car/types.ts`) holds its place on the wheels' plane (`x`, `y`, `z`), its heading as a unit vector (`hx`,
`hz`), its velocity as its speed along the heading and its slip to the right of it, the steering as a tangent, the controls last taken,
its lights, the steps it owes (`owed`, above) and four seats holding a player's id or 0. Seat 0 is the driver's (front left), 1 the front right, 2 and 3
the rear.

A player carries a `Ride` beside the engine's record (`Walker` is the engine's `PlayerCore` with a `ride`): the car's
id, a seat and a phase, which is on foot, getting in, seated or getting out, with the ticks that phase has left. A
player getting in or out is held still (`carHeld`); a seated player is put at the seat's place every tick, 0.5 m over
the wheels' plane.

### The step

`stepCar` (`sim/car/step.ts`) is one tick of the car on its own, reading no world: the ground arrives as a `CarEnv` (a
top speed, a grip and the slope along the heading). The car is a single-track (bicycle) model. The heading is a unit
vector turned by adding its right-hand perpendicular times the turn rate and making it a unit vector again, so there is
no trigonometry and no angle is held; the front wheels' angle is the tangent `steer`, which the driver's turn input sets
as a target (the lock, 0.625, falls to half at 14 m/s over the ground and the wheels turn to it at 2.2 per second and
return at 3.5) and the turn rate is `speed * steer / WHEELBASE`.

The car's velocity is its own. A turn of the heading leaves the velocity where it pointed (it is taken apart again
along the new heading and across it), and the tyres then take the sideways part away at the rate the grip allows. While
the grip can take all of it the car goes where it points, keeps its speed, and `slip` is 0. A turn that asks for more
sideways acceleration than the grip turns the nose by the grip's share and by 0.16 of the excess (`YAW_FOLLOW`), the
rear cannot hold that, and the car slides with its nose inside its path: the slide a car is steered into. Four rules
shape it:

- **Sliding tyres hold less.** The grip falls to 0.8 of itself (`SLIDE_GRIP`) as the slide grows to 3 m/s
  (`SLIDE_FULL`), and the rear's by a further quarter at full throttle (`POWER_SLIDE`), so a slide is easier to keep
  than to start and the throttle holds it.
- **The handbrake lets the rear go.** It leaves the rear 0.35 of its grip, the car pivots on its front wheels (the turn
  rate is 1.2 times the bicycle's, and the nose follows 0.45 of the excess), and it brakes at 4.5 m/s^2: the car is
  thrown round, tail out.
- **The steering helps.** Above 3 m/s forward, and with the handbrake off, the front wheels are turned toward where the
  car is going by 1.5 times the slide's tangent (the slip over the speed; `COUNTER_STEER`), by no more than a tangent of
  0.25 and by as much as the ground's grip is of the pavement's. This is what holds a steered-into slide small (about 9
  degrees at full lock at 10 m/s on pavement, 7 at 20 and 4 at 30), what brings a slide straight when the wheel is let
  go (within a second and a half of a handbrake turn at any speed), and why the car is looser on gravel.
- **A slide gives no speed.** The lock is narrowed by the speed over the ground, not along the heading, so a slide that
  turns the car's speed sideways opens no lock; and with no throttle the speed over the ground only falls.

Along the heading the engine gives up to `ACCEL` (6.7 m/s^2) times the throttle, falling to nothing at the surface's top
speed; the brake takes 9, a car at rest in reverse gathers 5 m/s^2 to 6 m/s, and rolling resistance 1.2. Over a
surface's top speed a car is dragged back at 4. The slope along the heading adds `GRAVITY * slope`; a slope steeper than
the climb limit (0.45) only slows a car that is moving forward, and a car at rest with no throttle stays at rest.

The designed top speeds (`TOP_SPEED` in `tuning.ts`) and grips (`GRIP`) are by surface:

| Surface | Top speed (m/s) | Grip (m/s^2) |
|---|---|---|
| paved | 30 | 8.3 |
| gravel | 20 | 5.4 |
| two wheel tracks | 12 | 4.9 |
| open ground | 6 | 3.9 |

The speed is also held to a limit by place (`speedLimitAt`, `sim/car/limit.ts`): `TOWN_SPEED`, 10 m/s, where the town's
cover holds the ground, rising smoothly to `FULL_SPEED`, 15 m/s, over the cover's fade past the town's last blocks
(`COVER_FADE`, 160 m), and 15 m/s beyond. It follows the blocks and the quay and not the single outlying lots, so a lone
building by the highway does not slow the car, and it is a pure function of the seed and the place, so the host's step
and a joined machine's prediction agree. The streamed world does not keep up with an eye that travels faster than
that; the table keeps the designed speeds for when the world is made ahead of travel, and raising `FULL_SPEED` is the
whole change. `carEnv` (`sim/car/body.ts`) takes the mean of the four wheels' surfaces and holds the top speed to the
lesser of that and the place's limit. A car over the limit is slowed by the drag over the top speed, never stopped.

### What is under a wheel

`surfaceAt(seed, x, z)` (`sim/car/surface.ts`) answers paved, gravel, tracks or open for a place; the first match wins.
A deck of the harbour is paved; so is the highway's bed (`ROAD_BED_HALF` of its centre). Within half a street's roadway
of its centre line the ground is paved, or gravel for an alley (paved wins where the two meet), and a stair or a rail is
no road. Within half the width of a road out, it is the surface of the nearer end of that road's segment. Inside a paved
ground polygon it is gravel on the dock end, the mill's site and the industrial flats and paved elsewhere. Anything else
is open ground. A grid of 16 m cells over the streets, roads and polygons, made once per world seed, names what is near
a place, so a lookup reads one cell.

### What stops the car

The footprint is three circles of 0.92 m along the car's axis, at -1.58, 0 and +1.58 m (`sim/car/body.ts`). `driveCar`
is one whole tick: the environment, `stepCar`, then each circle is moved out of everything solid, up to four passes. The
town's buildings and props are pushed out of by `constrainCircle` (`sim/town/solid.ts`), which reads every grid cell the
circle reaches and so meets what a circle wider than a walker's hull overlaps; the forest's boxes that stand as walls
(its trees) are pushed out of as boxes, and the harbour's rails and the railroad's trestle guard rails hold the car as
they hold a walker, each circle at its own radius (`constrainRails` and `constrainTrestle` take the hull's radius, which is
the walker's by default), so the body stops at a rail and the outer wheels stay on the deck. A push moves the whole car and takes the part of its velocity that points into the wall. A box whose
top is no more than 0.35 m over the ground at the circle is driven over, and so is one that begins above the car's roof.

Two rules undo the tick whole: a move that leaves a wheel deeper in water than `WADE` (0.3 m) and deeper than the
deepest wheel was where the tick began, and a move on to ground steeper than the climb limit along the heading, in
either direction, and steeper than where the tick began. The car is then put back as the tick found it, at rest, so a
car somewhere it should not be can always move toward gentler ground or shallower water. The car's height is the mean of
its wheels' ground. Walkers are pushed out of every car's footprint (`constrainByCars`, a rotated rectangle) first, and then
the town's own `constrain` (the props, then the walls) puts them out of whatever they were pushed into, as props are
pushed out before walls: a walker pinned by a moving car against a wall ends the tick outside the wall. A car is not
stopped by a walker.

### What gives way

Some of what stands in the town is not a wall to the car: the signs at the junctions (STOP, DEAD END, PAVEMENT ENDS and
the street-name blades, but not the sign on a planter), a sidewalk's trash cans that stand, its newspaper boxes, its
sign posts and its benches, the wheeled carts that stand, the drums, the stacks of pallets, and the mailboxes, on a
post or in a cluster. `knockablesFor(seed)` (`sim/town/knockables.ts`) lists them for a town, each a point in the world
with a radius (0.1 to 0.34 m) or, for the long ones (a bench, a stack of pallets, a cluster of mailboxes), the oriented
box the props' solids hold of it, and a place in the list that is its id, in a fixed order (the signs, then the
furniture, the bins and the mailboxes, each in the plan's order), with a grid of 16 m cells over them. Where the
junctions' signs stand is one list, `junctionSigns` (`sim/town/junctionSigns.ts`), that the drawing builds each sign
from and the knockables are read from, so the two cannot disagree. A hydrant, a planter, a dumpster, a parked vehicle,
a utility pole and everything larger stop the car as before.

The car is not pushed out of one. The carts, drums, pallets, benches and mailboxes are boxes of the props' solids, which
a walker is still held out of; the car's own grid of solids (`carPropSolids`, `propSolid.ts`) leaves them out. On the
tick the car's body (its rectangle, against a round thing's circle or a long thing's box) comes to touch one it did not
touch as the tick began, and the car is going 0.5 m/s or more over the ground, it is slowed (`knockCar`,
`sim/car/knock.ts`, the last thing `driveCar` does before it stands the car on the ground): it keeps a share of its
speed and loses a little more, along its heading and across it alike, so a hit throws its line no way but back.

| What | Share of the speed taken | And then (m/s) |
|---|---|---|
| a bench | 0.18 | 0.9 |
| a cluster of mailboxes | 0.15 | 0.8 |
| a stack of pallets | 0.14 | 0.7 |
| a junction's sign | 0.12 | 0.6 |
| a sidewalk's sign post, a newspaper box, a drum | 0.10 | 0.5 |
| a mailbox on a post | 0.08 | 0.4 |
| a trash can | 0.06 | 0.3 |
| a wheeled cart | 0.05 | 0.25 |

The rule keeps nothing. The hit is read from the car as the tick found it and as the tick leaves it, so `driveCar` is
still a pure function of the world's seed and the car, the same on the host, in a client's prediction and in its
replay, and nothing was added to the wire. What it does not keep is whether the thing still stands: a car that comes
back over what it knocked down is slowed again.

That the thing lies down is the drawing's (`game/town/knocked.ts`), worked out each frame on each screen from the cars
as they are drawn: a thing a moving car's body touches goes down away from the car (along its velocity, turned outward
by the side of the car it was met on), tipping over its own edge in 0.42 s (a long thing over whichever of its edges is
toward the fall), lies for 12 s, and stands back up over 0.9 s, but not while a car is on it. No more than eight are
down at once; one more, and the one that has lain longest stands at once. A hit is heard as a thud, louder the faster
the car (`side.ts`).

### What is struck

A building, a dumpster and a parked vehicle stop the car as they always did, and the simulation keeps nothing of the
blow. What is left to see is the drawing's (`game/town/struck.ts`), worked out each frame on each screen as what is
knocked down is. A car that loses more speed over the ground than its brakes could take (22 m/s^2), while one of its
three circles is within 0.15 m of a solid's face that it was moving into, has struck it; a stop that is drawn over two
frames or three is added up and lands once, as hard as the whole of it, and under 0.6 m/s lost is no blow.

- **A mark.** Where the car touched, 0.55 m over the ground its wheels stand on and 3 cm off the face, a mark of damage
  is left: a dark bruise with pale cracks running out of it, the pale scrape of the bumper across it and a scatter of
  pale chips, 0.9 m across for the least blow to 1.8 m for 8 m/s lost. It lasts 25 s and then closes up over 1.5 s; no
  more than sixteen show at once. The marks are one small see-through mesh drawn as thin instances
  (`game/town/damageParts.ts`), each laid flat on the face that was struck.
- **A shove.** A dumpster or a parked vehicle is also moved: slid the way the car was going, 2 cm for each m/s lost, no
  more than 12 cm in a blow and 30 cm in all, and rocked, leaning away from the blow by up to 4 degrees and back, dying
  out in about a second. It stays where it was pushed for 25 s and is then eased back over 2 s to where the world has
  it, since the simulation's box of it never moved. The marks on it move with it. No more than four are shoved at once.

### How the town's batches move

The things that give way, the dumpsters and the parked vehicles that are built as geometry are part of the town's
batches, which are not rebuilt. As a door's leaf is, each is laid with a tag in its vertices' colour alpha (`withKnock`,
`KNOCK_ALPHA` 0.05, between a leaf's and the least a rank of paint has), and the town's vertex stage (`KNOCK_VERTEX`,
`townMaterial.ts`) moves a tagged vertex that stands in a moved thing's box: it is turned about the horizontal line
through the edge the thing leans over, at the ground, and then slid, by uniforms the frame writes (`writeKnocked`: up to
twelve things, a knocked one's fall or a shoved one's rock and slide). `knockMatrix` is the same arithmetic as a matrix,
for what the stage does not draw: a parked vehicle that is a model is drawn moved by its own instance matrix
(`VehicleParts.moved`), and a mark on a shoved thing is carried by it.

### Seats, doors, getting in and out

A door is the place 0.5 m outside the body beside a seat (`sim/car/seats.ts`); a walker within 1.2 m of one can use it.
Getting in takes the driver's seat if it is empty, else the seat of the door used, else nothing, and needs the car under
2 m/s over the ground, along its heading and sliding across it (`LEAVE_SPEED`, `slowForDoors`), and the walker's feet within 1 m (`DOOR_HEIGHT`) of the car's wheels' plane, so no one gets in from
under a pier the car stands on, and no one gets in beside a moving car and is carried off from the door (`boardSeat`; the
line at the door reads the same function, so it offers a seat only where a press would take one). Getting in takes 60
ticks, getting out 36. Getting out needs the car under 2 m/s and a free place to stand at the seat's own door or the one
across: clear of buildings, props, cars and rails, in no more than wading water, and on ground within 0.6 m (`RAIL_JOIN`,
the decks' own join) of the car's height, so a car beside a railed deck edge puts its rider out on the deck, or keeps
them seated, and never on the ground metres below.

The host alone decides these (`carPressed`, `carHostStep`, from the engine's `pressed` and `hostStep`). A press of the
interact bit on foot at a door takes the seat that tick, so two players cannot share one; the countdown, the completion
of getting in, the placing of a walker at the door, the emptying of a seat whose player has left the game and the car
with no driver (held at rest by the handbrake) are the host's, and so are the lamps (the lamp bit from the driver flips
the car's lights) and the command that brings the car back. If a player leaving finds the car moving again or no place
to stand, they stay seated.

### The wire

`net.ts` is the game's `NetGame`, at wire version `WALK_PROTOCOL` 3. A snapshot is a header (the message type, the tick,
the last input taken and the count of players, 10 bytes), the players (31 bytes each: the engine's id, place, velocity,
look and grounded flag, then the ride: a car's id, a seat, a phase and its ticks, then the room: its id, a phase and its ticks, see "Walking into a building"), a count of cars and the cars (35
bytes each, at these offsets: the id 0, the place 1 to 12, the heading as two 16-bit integers 13 to 16, the speed and the
slip 17 to 20, the steer 21 to 22, the throttle 23 and the turn 24 as bytes, a flags byte for the handbrake and the lights
25, the four seats 26 to 33, and the steps the car owes 34). Headings are made unit vectors again after they are read.

The host's snapshot is the truth the client is corrected to: `correct` sets the local player's ride and replaces every
car of the predicted world with the snapshot's, and the client's replay of its unacknowledged commands then drives the
car as the host did. A snapshot comes from another machine, so a read checks what it takes in (`decodeWalkSnapshot`):
the length must be exact, a ride's phase must be one of the four, a rider must name a car and one of four seats, and no
two riders may hold one seat, a car's heading must not be zero and the steps it owes must not pass `OWED_MAX`; a snapshot
that fails throws. A rider whose car the snapshot does not hold is on foot. For
drawing, `blend` blends the cars between two snapshots (the place, and the heading made a unit vector again), and `view`
gives the car the local player drives from the predicted world, every other car from the blended pair, and puts a
passenger at their seat in the car as it is drawn.

### How it is drawn

`client/src/game/car/carParts.ts` draws the real sedan (`vehicle.sedan_a`, the parked sedans' own file and material) on
every tier, including the low tier whose parked vehicles are code-built. Each role of the model is a mesh of thin
instances rewritten every frame, up to four cars. The engine's loader puts a mirror of x on a model's root, which leaves
the sedan's nose at the tail, so the car is drawn from the model's vertices turned half a circle about its up
(`halfTurnAboutY`, `carPose.ts`); the manifest's wheel places hold after it. The `tyre` mesh is cut into four wheels by
the manifest's wheel centres (`splitWheels`), so each rolls with the speed and the front pair turns with the steering.
The body is stood on the plane fitted through the drawn ground under its four wheels and raised by whatever the worst
wheel is sunk under it. The simulation's car is rigid, and the body that is drawn rides on springs over it
(`bodySway.ts`): how the car's velocity changed since the last frame is the acceleration the body felt, whose part
along the heading pitches it (the nose dips under the brake, 2.3 degrees at 9 m/s^2, and lifts under the engine) and
whose part across rolls it (out of a turn, 4.3 degrees at the pavement's grip), and the rise and fall of the wheels'
height carries it up and down after them, slower back up than down. The roll and the pitch are underdamped, so a car
that stops short nods once; a change of velocity too sudden to be the tyres' (over 22 m/s^2) is a hit, and throws the
body toward what it struck. Only the body's meshes are moved: the wheels and the dark patch stay on the ground, and
nothing the rules read changes. The car the local player drives is drawn `alpha` of the way between the last two ticks, as the
eye is, so car and camera move as one; a car further than a jump from its last place is not blended. A dark patch of the
ground's under the car is a see-through mesh drawn where the town's own dark at the walls' feet is. A model that does
not load leaves no car drawn and one warning, and the camera follows all the same.

### The camera

While the local player is in the car (getting in, seated or getting out) the eye is `stepChase` (`chaseCamera.ts`): a
chase view 3.25 m behind and 1.95 m over the car at rest, aimed 1.5 m over it, on a point that trails the car at a rate
and snaps to it where the car jumps. The mouse looks around it; after two seconds without the mouse it eases back behind
the car. The camera eases between the walker's eye and the chase over how far the player is
into the seat, which follows the ride's countdown at a bounded rate, so getting in and out is a smooth move and a
refused leave eases back.

"Behind" is behind where the car is going, part of the way. In a slide the velocity points to one side of the nose, and
the camera comes round toward it by 0.55 of that angle, so the car is seen turned in the frame and the road it slides
along stays ahead. A car that has backed for 0.7 s is followed from its nose, looking the way it goes, until it goes
forward or has stood for 0.6 s. C, held, turns the view half a circle at once. The speed over the ground, as a share of
the fastest the car goes (`CHASE_FAST`, the lesser of the pavement's top speed and `FULL_SPEED`) and eased over about
half a second, widens the lens by up to 0.15 rad and stands the camera up to 0.7 m further back. A fall in speed too
sudden for the brakes (over 22 m/s^2) is a hit and shakes the view for about half a second.

It is stopped short of what it would pass through: a building, a prop's box that is at least 0.6 m across, another car,
and the drawn ground (a sample of the sweep under the ground, with 0.1 m to spare, is blocked, so a crest between the
camera and the car pulls it in; and it stays 0.4 m over the ground where it stands). It goes in at once and comes back out at 4 m/s, and never nearer than 1.5 m to
the aim point.

V switches the cinematic view on and off for a seated player (`cineCamera.ts`, `viewKeys.ts`): a run of shots cut from
one to the next, with the chase stepped under it so the view comes back to a camera already behind the car. A roadside
camera stands still ahead of the car (12 m and 1.5 s of its travel, to 60 m), beside its line, low or at head height or
on a pole, turns to follow it and holds it at one size by its lens (0.3 rad while it is far, to 1 as it passes), until
the car is 26 m past, out of sight for 0.3 s, or 7 s are up. Between roadside shots come one fixed low beside a front
wheel looking forward, one from above and behind (which stands only where no building is in the way), and one ahead of
the car looking back; a car that stands is turned round slowly; and where nothing else stands, a low shot behind the
car, drawn in as the chase is. Every shot reads the chase's own `blocked`, so none stands in a building, a vehicle or
the ground and none looks at the car through one. The run is drawn from the count of cuts, so it is the same for the
same drive. The keys are the screen's own and no part of the command the simulation is sent: they are read only while
the pointer is locked to the game and not typed into a field, and a touch player has neither.

### The lamp

The engine has one lamp, the walker's own. While the local player is seated in a car (any seat) with its lights on, that
lamp is the car's headlights (`headlights.ts`): moved to 2.3 m ahead of the car's centre and 0.65 m up, pointing along
the heading a little down, reaching 40 m, and six times the walker lamp's brightness. The page's lamp button, and F,
switch the lights when the player drives; the button reads lit while the car's lights are.

### The sound

`carSound.ts` is the game's part of the engine's audio bus: an engine note (three gears over 0 to 30 m/s), tyre noise
whose colour follows the surface under the car and which rises to its loudest and brightest as the car slides (5 m/s
sideways), a thud when a car's speed over the ground falls hard in one tick, and a door when a
player gets in or out, all synthesised, on the bus the page's mute, hush and muffle already reach. The nearest car
within 80 m that has a driver or is moving is the one heard; a car with nobody at the wheel has tyres and no engine.

### The words and the command

`words.ts` holds what the game says at a door: "Click to get in", "Click to get out", and "No room to get out here",
which stands for 1.6 s in place of Get out when a click on a seated player in a slow car changes nothing for 0.3 s. The
line is `hud.ts`'s prompt, which is also pressable for a touch player. The command `carhome` (`/carhome` in the command
bar) puts the car back at its start, at rest with its lights off, and sets its riders on foot beside it; only the host
can say it, and a joined player is told so.

### For measuring tools

While the town's part lives, `globalThis.__capeCar` (`carDebug.ts`) answers with copies, never the game's own records:
`cars()` (each car as it was drawn), `self()` (the local player and ride), `camera()` (with the cinematic shot held, 0 for the chase), `knocked()` (the things this screen draws lying knocked down now, each with the angle it has fallen by and how long ago it was hit), `struck()` (the blows so far, the marks of damage that show and the things that stand shoved), `doors(id)`, `prompt()`, `lamp()`
(the one lamp as the last frame wrote it) and `sound()`.

### The car's limits

- The top speed is held to 10 m/s in the town and 15 m/s (`FULL_SPEED`) past it, by place (`speedLimitAt`), because the
  streamed world does not keep up with a faster eye; the designed speeds stay in the table for when it does.
- The car that is simulated is rigid: on a curb it tilts and a wheel can hang in the air. The suspension is drawn
  only (the body leans and rides over wheels that stay where the rigid car has them), and the wheels do not travel in
  their arches.
- The handling was tuned by its numbers at 10, 20 and 30 m/s (`client/test/car/step.test.ts` holds the shape: the small
  slide at full lock, the handbrake's throw, the recovery), and driven only at the 10 m/s the cap allows.
- The view's keys (C and V) are the keyboard's: a touch player has no look behind and no cinematic view.
- A wall met at a steep angle stops the car until it is steered or reversed; no hit turns the car.
- What a car knocks down, shoves or marks is drawn by each screen from what it sees: it is not in the world's state, so
  a player who joins later sees it as it was built, a walker is not stopped by a fallen sign or can (and is still
  stopped by a fallen cart, bench or mailbox, as by a standing one), and the car is slowed again by a thing it runs
  over while it lies there. A renderer that is made again (a window resized) stands everything up and clears the marks.
- A thing falls as one stiff piece and lies where it fell: nothing rolls, bends, breaks apart or is carried along by
  the car. Two things that stand within one another's box move together (under one tagged vertex in twenty-five is in
  two boxes: a cart against a parked car, a can beside a bench).
- A shoved dumpster or vehicle is moved on the screen only: the box the car is stopped by stays where the world has it,
  up to 30 cm from what is drawn, until the thing is eased back.
- A mark is flat, on the box of what was struck: on a vehicle it stands a little off a curved body, and on a wall it
  may lie over a window or a door as over brick. Trees, utility poles, fences and the harbour's rails take no mark.
- On the low tier a newspaper box and a sidewalk's sign post are not drawn, and still slow the car.
- The camera's sweep treats a building as a column to the sky and a prop's box as a column 4 m over the ground (they
  carry no heights), so a low bin or wall pulls it in as a tall one would.
- There is one lamp: only the local player's own car lights the road for that player, and the lights of other cars are
  not seen.
- The door sounds for the local player only, and the voice keeps sounding with the pause menu up.
- The "no room to get out" line is decided by a timer on the player's own machine, so it can show wrongly to a joined
  player on a slow link, before the host's answer has come back.
- `?cmd=carhome` in the address does nothing; the command typed in the command bar works.

## Walking into a building

The bar, the cafe, the lodging and the municipal building (where the player's office is) can be walked into: the first three are rooms of the plan, the fourth is made from the building's frame (below). A click
at a building's street door holds the player still while the screen goes dark, the host then moves the player into a copy of
the room, and a click at the room's own door fades back out to the street. The buildings stay solid to everyone outside: no
one in the street ever sees a room, and no one in a room sees the street.

### The hidden room

A room a player stands in is not the shop behind the street's glass. It is the same room, as the plan furnished it
(`Unit.interior`), built again `LIFT` = 60 m over the street at the same place on the map, so the room's frame is its
unit's front wall in the world and its floor is the walked ground at its street door plus 60 m. It is 60 m up for three
reasons. Nothing of the town reaches that high, so no roof, tree or crane stands in a room. The engine's rain cover
reaches 100 m up, so a roof over the room still stops the rain there. And it is not under the ground, where the engine's
height fog and its water line would be wrong for a player below the sea. The rooms are `insideRooms(seed)`
(`client/src/sim/inside/rooms.ts`), a pure function of the world's seed: ids 1 the bar, 2 the cafe, 3 the lodging, each with
its walls, its door and its furnishings as rectangles.

### The phases

A player's place by the rooms is `Inside`: the room's id (0 in the street), a phase and the ticks left of a hold. The phases
are `OUTSIDE`, `GOING_IN`, `INSIDE` and `GOING_OUT`. It follows the car's pattern (`sim/car/rules.ts`): the interact bit is a
press on the host (`insidePressed`, from the `pressed` hook, which gives a player in the street to the car first and to the
door only if the car did not act); a held phase counts `DOOR_TICKS` (27, 450 ms) down in the host's step (`insideStep`),
during which the engine holds the player still (`held`); then the player is moved: to `arriveIn(room)` for going in, and
for going out to `arriveOut(room)` moved clear of what stands there by the town's own constraint, on the walked ground.
A player in a room moves by `insideMove`, the engine's own walking step on a flat floor with no jump (the eye would go
through a low ceiling), then `constrainInRoom` keeps the hull in the walls and out of the furnishings; where two pieces
of furniture stand closer than a hull is wide, the player stays where they were. It runs on the host, in a client's
prediction and in its replay, so it is deterministic from the room and the command. The line at a door ("Click to go in",
"Click to go out") asks the same `streetDoorAt` and `roomDoorAt` as the host's press, and shows nothing during a hold. The
screen's black cover (`WalkHud.setDark`: 200 ms to black, 450 ms to clear) follows the phase in the snapshot, going dark
in `GOING_IN` and `GOING_OUT`, never the click, so the screen is black before the player is moved.

### On the wire

Three bytes more for each player after the ride: the room's id, the phase and its ticks (`MOVER_BYTES` is 31, `WALK_PROTOCOL`
3). A read refuses a phase outside the four and a room's id that is 0 unless the phase is `OUTSIDE`. The host's word
corrects the local player's `inside` as it does the ride. `view` leaves out of the frame every other player who is not where
the local player is (the room's id for `INSIDE` and `GOING_OUT`, else the street), since a player in a room is 60 m up and
must not be drawn to someone in the street, nor the reverse. The console's `goin bar`, `goin cafe`, `goin lodging` and
`goin out` put the host's player in a room, with no hold, for looking at one; from an address it waits for the world and
the player.

### How it is drawn

**The fourth room, the municipal building's** (id 4, name "office", `unit` -1), has no plan record to be built from, and the plan must not change (its digests are pinned), so its inside is made by
`sim/inside/municipal.ts` from the building's front frame alone (`municipalInterior(seed)`, a pure function of the seed): an `Interior` in the plan's own types (type "lobby", the building's whole front wide,
`min(14, frame.depth - 1.5)` deep, 3.6 m high) with the street door (the place's pair of doors, 2.3 m, at the middle `C`), the city clerk's counter on the left and the sheriff's on the right with desks and
cabinets behind, a partition across at d 7.2 with two doorways (into the player's office at `C - 2.4`, and a shut door, solid, at `C + 3.0`), the player's office behind it (a desk, a couch, cabinets, a plant, a table
and a lamp), benches, plants, a water cooler, a low table and a flag in the hall, tube lights, the fixed places and activities of its people (a clerk, a deputy, one or two visitors), and the stations of the front windows as the
street side lays them out. Nothing in the hall, the way from the door to the office's doorway and the door's zone is built on (a test holds it). Nothing in it names an outside service. `rooms.ts` makes the room from it as
for the others (a partition and the shut door are solids). Its drawing is the same whole-room path with a neutral trade for its dressing (the bank's wooden floor and wainscot, and what an office hangs), hours "always", and
`municipalDecor.ts` for what the kit has not: boards over the counters lettered "SHERIFF" and "CITY CLERK" and a plate "ROOM 3" by the office's doorway (in the town's stroke font, `signFont.ts`, laid as quads), the doorways' frames and
the shut door's leaf, a notice board, a clock, a flag on its stand and a cork board with a map in the office. The windows are the street side's stations (`plainFront`) at its sill and head heights, in the look the street rolls for each; the street side
leaves out a window the ground stands too high for, this does not.

The hidden room is drawn by `insideGeometry.ts` and `insideParts.ts` (`client/src/game/town/`).

- **Built whole, from the same record.** `insideGeometry(g, room, detail)` calls `roomGeometry`, the builder of the room behind the street's glass,
  with the unit's own `Unit.interior`, state, hours and finish (so a shop shut for the night is as dark inside as it looks from the street), the
  building's own frame (`buildingLevels(...).xf`, which a test holds equal to the room's frame to a centimetre) and a floor height such that the drawn floor (the shell draws it
  8 cm over its `floorY`) is `room.floorY`, where the player's feet stand. `RoomIn.whole` builds it as `through` builds a room (to its back wall, with no painted sheet and no haze)
  and leaves nothing out for being unseen from the street: `fbox`, `cbox`, `fcyl`, `wallBox` and the shelves' goods draw every side, a box held off the floor has its
  underside and a ceiling thing its top, a face that lies in a wall's own plane is left out (it would fight the wall), and a front wall is built on the inside at 15 cm from the unit's openings and door
  (`frontWall` in `roomShell.ts`): the wall's bands cut round the holes, each window a bright pane with its frame bars (slats, paper or boards where the plan covers it) and the door shut in its frame with a pull. A pane glows
  with the day and is dark at night (the hours code 0, the day's glow: `DAY_GLOW_CODE` in `townMaterial.ts`); it shows no street.
- **Every face has its back.** The builder given `new MeshBuilder(true)` lays the back of everything it lays (the same corners wound the other way, normals reversed, look unchanged), so no
  face is see-through from any side whichever builder laid it; the hidden room is built in that mode and its triangles come in pairs. The street's build is not (`MeshBuilder()` is as it was).
- **Its own material.** `insideParts.ts` makes the room one mesh in the town's solid material without the wet look (a floor indoors is not wet); the engine's air stays on it, since on the tiers with
  no post chain it also carries the display curve and the grade. The room is built when the local player is `INSIDE` or `GOING_OUT`, or in `GOING_IN` once the hold is past 12 ticks (the screen is dark), at once for a
  player who is simply `INSIDE`, and let go the frame the phase is `OUTSIDE`: a room is never kept while the player is in the street.
- **Rain and sound indoors** (`game/insideWeather.ts`, from `walkSide.beforeSync`). The engine knows nothing of indoors, and two of its rain draws ignore a roof. The lens (the droplets the post chain refracts the frame through) is a
  function of the weather's rain and the view's pitch alone, with no cover in it. The drips are drawn *under* hard cover (alpha is covered x (1 - canopy) x the canopy's water, a state a minute of rain fills and ten of dry drain),
  so a roof over the room would show them. So the room is not registered as cover, and from the start of the hold at the street door to the start of the way out the local view's weather is the real weather with no rain
  (`setView`, at once; a console `weather`, a renderer made again and the clock's pushes of the hour keep it), the world's beds are cut by half (`setHush`) and put back going out. Only the local view changes, and the street's
  wetness is not touched. The lens eases out over its own second (`lensSmooth`), so a little of it is left when the screen clears.
- **The town is hidden while inside.** While the phase is `INSIDE`, `townParts.seen` shows none of the town's own batches (they are below the floor), nor to the rain's map; in `GOING_OUT` they are drawn again so the street is there when the screen clears.
- **People.** `insidePeople` stands copies of each room's unit's people in the hidden room (`roomPeople`, `people.ts`: the same placement as the street's, over the whole room to the back wall less a margin
  and not the front 2 m), tagged with the room's id; `peopleParts` shows a person with a room only while the local player is where that room is (`setInside`), and then shows nobody else, walkers and the
  street's people included. Their bodies are part of the people's capacity.
- **Seen close.** In a whole room the things that read as lit screens from the street are drawn as what they are: a television is a dark frame round a dim picture, a jukebox or a vending machine a
  coloured panel with darker bars and buttons (or lit goods behind dark glass), a dartboard has its wedges, rings and bull at a real size, a beer sign a label with lettering, the kitchen's pass-through a dim warm back, a lobby's lamps
  stand (a foot, a stand and a shade) in place of a glowing box at the height of the shade. The street's build is as it was.
- **Limits.** The deeper part of a room keeps what the street's build puts there (no chairs past `PLAIN_FROM`, and the clutter the front 4 m have); a pane's glow is a flat sheet, not the day's light
  falling in; the room is lit by its own baked light only.

## The title page

Built in `client/src/game/` in the visual language of Day Hike's title page (the same dark ground and vignette, the
monospace type, the filled and hollow buttons, the panel slide, the focus and `inert` handling), with no background still
or loop and no downloads, join box or update banner, since there is no desktop app.

- `words.ts` holds every word the page says that is the game's own, as constants.
- `landingModel.ts` decides what the page shows, as data, so the decisions are tested without a DOM: the quiet line
  `In development.` on every build while the game is that, and under it Play, `Loading…` while the world builds, or
  `Waiting for the host…` for a follower. Play is offered whether or not the build was given a signaling address: with no
  server the game is played alone, and the engine's shell shows no party.
- `landing.ts` paints the model with DOM APIs only (`textContent`, never `innerHTML`) and slides between the home panel and
  the Settings and Credits panels. Settings and Credits are the engine's screens (`renderSettings`, `renderCredits`).
- The panels have addresses, `/settings` and `/credits`, which the shell routes: `showPanel` slides the page to the panel,
  so the browser's Back closes it.
- `credits.ts` merges the repository's `CREDITS.md` (no entry yet) with the engine's `ENGINE_CREDITS`, in the order of the
  ids, then the engine's display pass.

## Hosting

The client is a static Vite build under the base `/capesorrow/` (`CAPESORROW_BASE`), published to its path on the shared
Firebase Hosting site `games.csarko.sh`, which Day Hike's repository owns. The deploy (`tools/deploy/client.mjs`) uses the
engine's shared-site tool, which merges this game's files and rules (`tools/deploy/site.rules.json`: a rewrite of every
address under `/capesorrow/` to its page, and an immutable cache header on `/capesorrow/assets/`, where Vite puts the
hashed files) into the live site without touching another game's. `tools/deploy/verify.mjs` checks the result from
outside, including that Day Hike is as it was. The map page is not in the build. There is no server deployed and no
infrastructure of this game's own, so the public page says `In development.` and Play is offered in development only.

## What is still scaffold

`client/src/assets.ts` and `client/levels/yard.json` are the engine's empty game as its `tools/new-game.mjs` wrote them
and are unchanged: the engine's shipped trees, cliffs and sounds (the game has no shipped files of its own), and the
level a forest world must carry, with no brushes. `hud.ts` and `net.ts` are the scaffold's files with the car added:
the HUD is a line of status and the line at a car's door, the snapshot is the players' places and the cars. The walking
rules keep the engine's names (`WALK_*`, `EMPTY_GAME`), and `sim.ts`, `game.ts` and `world.worker.ts`, which now carry
the town and the car, are still the scaffold's files in shape. They are to be replaced by Cape Sorrow's own rules and
parts: there is no case and no people, the only sound of the game's own is the car's, and there is nothing to do in the
town but walk and drive the one car. Likewise the favicon is a placeholder, and `CREDITS.md` has no entry because the
game has no third-party work of its own yet.
